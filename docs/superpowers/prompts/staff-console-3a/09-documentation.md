## Restoring

Restore the database and the `media-store` volume from the same moment. The API's sweeper deletes images that have no row: after a database restored from an older snapshot it would delete every image posted since. It refuses when there are many such images, but for a restore window set `MEDIA_RECONCILE=off` in `infra/.env` before starting the API, and put it back to `on` once the store matches the database. `force` makes one pass delete them anyway, for when they really are orphans.

The staff steps run with the api service stopped (`docker compose -f infra/compose.yml stop api`), before it starts again, with the commands under Staff console:

1. Run `staff.js audit-head` on the restored database. Its place and hash go to the keeper with the restore's date: new rows will take the places lost with the backup's later rows, with other hashes, so every place kept after the backup would fail as a rewrite does. The keeper retires those places with that note, `audit-verify` runs from then on with the places at or before the restored one, and a failing place that no announced restore explains is a rewrite.
2. Run `staff.js revoke <email> all` for every account `staff.js keys` lists, since the restore brings back every staff key, staff session and enrolment link revoked or cancelled after the backup, and the auth events and audit rows that recorded those revocations are gone with it.
3. Run grant-role for every staff account with the role on your own list, and `user` for any other account `staff.js keys` lists, as after an API incident: it sets back any role changed after the backup and ends every session and staff session of the account, app sessions revoked after the backup included. Then compare the accounts `staff.js keys` lists with that list.
4. Each person enrols again, two keys each.

A suspect or revoked staff key is never cleared or revived.

## Posting and feed
Text posts up to 1000 characters, replies, quotes, likes, reposts and bookmarks; follows, blocks and mutes; a chronological Following timeline and a public Latest timeline; threads and profile pages. The web app's home is the timeline; sign-in lives at `/signin`.

- **Reading signed out:** Latest, threads and profiles are public. Following, bookmarks, likes, follower lists and privacy settings need a session.
- **Blocks** are enforced in every query, not just the interface: neither account sees the other while signed in, follows are removed both ways, and the blocked side gets `not_found` so nothing is revealed. Posts are public, so a block does not hide them from someone who signs out; the interface says so.
- **Mutes** are one-way and silent, and hide an account from your timelines only.
- **Liker lists are never exposed.** Counts are public; likes and bookmarks are private to their owner.
- **Write limits** per account, tiered by account age: in the first 24 hours 5 posts and 20 replies an hour and no reposts or quotes for 48 hours; afterwards 30 posts and 60 replies an hour. Everyone: 100 likes an hour, 50 follows an hour and 400 a day, 30 blocks or mutes an hour. Undo actions are never limited.
- **Deleting** blanks the text in the database and leaves a tombstone only where a thread or a quote needs it. There is no editing.

## Images
Posts, replies and quotes can carry up to four images, and every image needs a description of up to 1000 characters. The web composer takes JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and still GIF files up to 20 MB, and refuses HEIC with advice to export a JPEG.

- **Nothing of the original is kept.** The upload goes to the `media-uploads` volume, a separate worker container decodes it and writes fresh WebP files in two sizes (1024 and 2048 pixels on the long side) without any metadata to `media-store`, and the original is deleted once the image is ready.
- **The worker is boxed in.** It has no network, no credentials and no capabilities, runs as a non-root user on a read-only file system within 768 MiB, one CPU and 64 processes, and only reads `media-uploads`. Decoders outside the allowlist are blocked, and the pixel count is checked from the header before decoding: 50 MP, 16 MP for AVIF, 16383 pixels on a side.
- **Formats can be switched off** without a deploy, for example while a codec advisory is open: remove it from `MEDIA_FORMATS` in `infra/.env` and restart `api` and `worker`.
- **Who sees an image** follows its post: anyone for a visible post, with a public five-minute cache; only the author for a withheld or removed post, never cached; nobody once the post is deleted, and its files leave the disk.
- **Behind Cloudflare**, set Browser Cache TTL to "Respect Existing Headers", or browsers keep images for four hours whatever the server says.

## Explore
A ranked list of recent original posts, the third tab of Home (`/?tab=explore`). Its whole formula is published at `/explore/about`, printed from `EXPLORE_V1` in `packages/contracts/src/explore.ts`, the constant the ranking itself uses; changing any number means a new formula version with a dated entry in its change log.
- **What is ranked.** Visible top-level posts that are not quotes, from the last 48 hours, by live accounts that have not switched Explore off. Replies belong to their threads, and quotes stay out so that a mocking quote cannot put its target's post on a public page.
- **What counts.** Approving accounts: the distinct accounts other than the author that liked or reposted the post, each at least 48 hours old when it did, still live, the like or repost at least 10 minutes old. The score is approving accounts / (hours since posting + 2)^1.8; a post needs at least 3; one account has at most 2 posts in a ranking of 300. Replies, quotes, followers, the badge and reports are never read.
- **The refresh.** The API's `explore-refresh` job ranks at start and every five minutes under the shared job lock, and a round within 4.5 minutes of the last writes nothing, however many instances run. Each ranking is a numbered generation in `explore_runs` and `explore_items`; the two newest are kept so a reader can finish paging, and a round logs only its generation, its counts and its milliseconds.
- **Reading.** `GET /api/timeline/explore` shows only the order, never a score, count or position. Its cursor names the ranking and the last post read; a pruned ranking answers 409 `explore_refreshed`, and the app goes on from the newest ranking without repeating a post. Moderation states, the author's switch or deletion, blocks in both directions and mutes apply at every read. Signed out: the first 20 posts and no cursor. Every answer is `no-store`.
- **Authors.** Settings > Privacy has "Show my posts in Explore" (`POST` and `DELETE /api/me/explore`, on by default): off takes the posts out at the next read, on brings them back at the next refresh. `GET /api/posts/:id/explore` tells the author alone whether a post is in Explore and which rule keeps it out, never with a count, and the author's listed posts show "In Explore".
- **Tests.** A ranking is a function of the time it is computed at, so the integration suites rank in past epochs of their own (2020 to 2023), each test in its own span after emptying `explore_runs`.

## Staff console
The staff console (`apps/admin`; design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-28-staff-console-3a-design.md`) is a page on an origin of its own, `API_CONSOLE_ORIGIN` in `infra/.env`, served by Caddy's second site (port 8090 in its container), which proxies only `/api/staff/*` to the API. Empty, the console is off: every `/api/staff` path answers 404, and the page says that the console is not available at this address. Only a staff key opens it. It holds sign-in, enrolment, a home screen (every staff account's sign-ins of the last 7 days, your staff sessions, your keys) and the audit log; cases arrive in sub-project 3c. Keys are enrolled, listed and revoked, and the record is checked, on the server:
```bash
docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm migrate node dist/staff.js enroll <email>        # a one-time link that registers one staff key
docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm api node dist/staff.js keys [<email>]             # an account's staff keys, or every staff account with its keys
docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm api node dist/staff.js revoke <email> <key id>    # that key and every staff session it opened
docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm api node dist/staff.js revoke <email> all         # every key, staff session and unused enrolment link of the account
docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm api node dist/staff.js audit-head                 # the audit log's newest place, hash and time
docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm api node dist/staff.js audit-verify [<chain_seq>:<hash> ...]   # the whole chain, against the places kept
```
Each command waits at most 10 seconds for a lock. One that gives up has changed nothing and says so: an open transaction holds the audit head or the account's row, so stop the api service (`docker compose -f infra/compose.yml stop api`) before running it again.

- **Staff keys.** A staff key is a WebAuthn credential registered on the console's relying party ID (`API_CONSOLE_RP_ID`; empty means the console's hostname) whose authenticator reported user verification and no backup eligibility when it was registered, and reports user verification without the backup flags at every use. A personal passkey never opens the console and a staff key never signs in to the app; passwords, email codes, two-factor and recovery codes never give staff powers. Keys are added only through enrolment links the command line issues as the migrator, which the database enforces, and an account holds at most 5 live keys. No console route adds or revokes one: `staff.js revoke` revokes keys, grant-role revokes them on a change to `user`, an account's deletion revokes them, and a key whose signature counter goes backwards is switched off together with every staff session it opened. A key's row is never deleted, because `audit-verify` checks old confirmations against its public key; a deleted account's keys keep no name, AAGUID or transports.
- **Enrolment.** `staff.js enroll` runs in the migrate service, as the migrator, the one role the running API never holds. It prints a link to `<API_CONSOLE_ORIGIN>/enroll/<token>`, valid 30 minutes and once, for a live account that is staff, has a handle and holds fewer than 5 live keys, and refuses while the console is off. Whoever holds the link can register a key for that account, so hand it over by a channel you trust. The person opens it on the device that holds the key, names the key (the key and the console show the name, so not an email address or a real name), and chooses "Register a security key" or "Use Windows Hello on this computer"; a refused key leaves the link working until it expires. The owner is mailed every key added. A link issued before a `revoke ... all`, a role change, a deletion or a move of the console enrols nothing.
- **Sessions and confirmations.** A staff session lasts at most 12 hours and ends after 15 minutes without an action by its person (a click, a submitted form, a screen opened); the console warns two minutes before the idle end with "Stay signed in", and nothing moves the 12-hour end. It is read from the database at every request, so a revocation, a demotion, a deletion or a switched-off key ends it at its next request. Every new staff session is mailed to its owner, and every staff member's home screen lists every staff account's sign-ins of the last 7 days: a sign-in nobody recognises means someone else holds a staff key. An action that changes something asks for the session's own key again, for that action, its target and its parameters only ("Sign out that session" in 3a, every decision from 3c), and its row in the audit log keeps the key's signature.
- **The audit log and its head.** The audit log is the record of every staff action: enrolment links issued and cancelled, keys added and revoked, sign-ins, sign-outs, session revocations and role changes, each written in the same transaction as its change, and from 3c every decision. It is a hash chain: the database gives every row its place (`chain_seq`) and a hash over the previous row's hash and the row's content, so a row changed later changes every hash after it. The console's audit page lists the rows newest first with their place and hash, with "Load older" and "Go to place". `audit-head` prints the newest row's place, hash and time (`<chain_seq>:<hash> <time>`); a kept place is its first word. `audit-verify` recomputes every hash in its own code, never asking the database for one, checks every stored confirmation against the public key, origin and relying party ID of the key it names, and checks every kept place given to it; it exits 1 naming the first rows at fault, and says how many rows follow the newest kept place. It needs no secret and runs the same with the console off or moved.
- **Making staff.** The person creates a korbat account of its own for staff work, at an address of its own (an alias of their mailbox will do, so the staff mails reach them), and chooses a handle unrelated to their posting handle; that account is used only for the console. It has a public profile under that handle, empty but visible in the app. The operator runs grant-role with `moderator` or `admin`, then `staff.js enroll` once per key, for two keys. The first admin is the operator, made the same way on an account apart from their posting account.
- **Offboarding.** `docker compose -f infra/compose.yml run --rm api node dist/grant-role.js <email> user` revokes the account's staff keys, staff sessions and unused enrolment links, and mails it the keys' names; a person made staff again enrols new keys. A change between `moderator` and `admin` ends the staff sessions and unused links and keeps the keys. A lost key is revoked with `staff.js revoke <email> <key id>` and replaced through a new enrolment; there is no self-service recovery.
- **Which keys work.** Out of the box only Windows offers a device-bound platform key (Windows Hello). On macOS, iOS, iPadOS, Android, ChromeOS and Linux the platform's own passkeys are synced, so a staff key there must be a FIDO2 security key with a PIN. On Windows a browser's password manager may offer itself before Windows Hello, and its passkeys are refused. A phone's own passkeys cannot serve, though a phone's browser can use a security key. The approved authenticators are a FIDO2 security key with a PIN and Windows Hello on a computer with a TPM, and each person holds two: two security keys, or Windows Hello on one computer and a security key. A new security key needs a PIN; when the browser does not offer to set one, set it in Chrome or Edge under Settings, Privacy and security, Security, Manage security keys, on Windows under Settings, Accounts, Sign-in options, Security key, or with the app of the key's maker.
- **Reading `staff.js keys`.** With an address it lists that account's keys; without one, every live account whose role is not `user`. Each account's line gives its role, address, handle and id; each key's line gives its id (what `revoke` takes), its name as a quoted string, its AAGUID, when it was created and last used, and whether it is live, revoked, or revoked and suspect (its counter went backwards). The AAGUID is the make and model the authenticator reported about itself, unsigned, since no attestation is asked: all zeros when the browser or the authenticator withheld it, `none` once the account's deletion blanked it. It shows what kind of authenticator registered the key without proving it, and a key whose AAGUID names a password manager is revoked.
- **A refused passkey.** When enrolment refuses a synced passkey or one that asked for no PIN, the password manager has already saved it. Where the browser can (Chrome and Edge 132 and later, Safari 26) and the console's relying party ID is not the app's, the page asks the browser to drop it. Otherwise the page says so, and the person deletes the entry for the console's hostname from the password manager's list of passkeys by hand. In development both relying party IDs are `localhost`: pick the entry by the key's name, so the app's own passkeys stay.
- **Staff mails.** None carries a link, and each ends with the sentence "korbat never puts a link in a staff email", so a staff mail with a link is a fake. "A staff key was added to your korbat staff account" is expected after your own enrolment; otherwise tell the operator at once, who revokes that key. "A staff session started on the korbat console" is expected after your own sign-in; otherwise someone holds that key: tell the operator at once, who revokes it with `staff.js revoke <email> <key id>`, which ends every session it opened. "A staff key on your korbat staff account was switched off" means a use of the key looked like a copy of it: ask the operator for a new enrolment link, and say whether you used the key just then, since if you did not, someone else holds a copy. "Staff keys revoked on your korbat staff account" follows an offboarding, a demotion, a revocation by the operator or a restore; if you did not expect it, ask the operator why. Signing out, or signing out one of your own sessions, sends no mail.

The runbook:

- **After each enrolment** the person presses "Sign in with this key" on the enrolment page, which signs in with the new key and no other. A key that does not open the console is revoked (`staff.js revoke <email> <key id>`) and the operator told: an API under someone else's control could have stored a key of its own in its place, under the person's name, and `staff.js keys` would not show the difference.
- **After any API incident** (the API's code, image or host was, or may have been, under someone else's control), stop the api service first: `docker compose -f infra/compose.yml stop api`. Run grant-role for every staff account with the role on your own list of staff: it sets back any role the API changed and ends every session and staff session, keeping the keys. Compare the accounts and addresses `staff.js keys` lists with that list; an account or an address that is not on it is made `user` with grant-role until it is explained. Once the API runs again from a clean image, run `audit-verify` with every kept place, and read the record's key and enrolment rows (`staff_enrollment_issued`, `staff_key_added`, `staff_key_revoked`, `staff_enrollments_cancelled`) on the console's audit page. Then each person signs in once with every key listed for them, and any key that does not open the console is revoked: `staff.js keys <email>` shows which listed key no sign-in used.
- **The kept head.** Until sub-project 3d publishes the head, each day the keeper, the staff member who does not run the server, copies the newest place and hash from the console's audit page and, on the same visit, opens the place kept on the previous visit with "Go to place" and checks that it still shows the kept hash. A change that recomputes the chain changes the hash at every later place, so the newest kept place covers every row before it. After each deploy and each week the operator runs `audit-verify` with every place the keeper has kept.
- **Moving the console** means enrolling every staff key again. Keys are bound to the console's hostname, and each records the origin and relying party ID it was enrolled for, which the API holds it to. After the move (a new `API_CONSOLE_ORIGIN`, the tunnel's console hostname, then `docker compose -f infra/compose.yml up -d api`, which recreates the API with it), run `staff.js revoke <email> all` for every staff account, since the old keys open nothing but still count towards the five, then `staff.js enroll` for each key again; links issued before the move are refused. `audit-verify` goes on checking old confirmations against the values recorded on each key.

## Profile and account
Signed-in accounts manage themselves under Settings; every route below needs the session and, where it says fresh, a re-authentication within five minutes (the app opens its panel when the API answers `reauth_required`).
- **Profile.** `PATCH /api/me/profile` sets the display name (up to 50 characters) and the bio (one line, up to 160). `POST /api/me/handle` (fresh) changes the handle once every 30 days; every handle an account ever had stays reserved for it in `handle_history`, so nobody else can take an old handle and the account can go back to it. There is no redirect from an old handle.
- **Email.** `POST /api/auth/email/change/start` (fresh) sends a code to the new address; `POST /api/auth/email/change/verify` moves the account there and tells the old address, whose notice carries a link (`/email-revert/<token>`, `POST /api/auth/email/revert`, valid 24 hours, once) that moves the account back and runs the lockdown described under Passkeys and fresh authentication (every session and device signed out, the password removed, passkeys and two-factor authentication set up since the day before the change removed). Sessions stay signed in after a change.
- **Verified badge.** Proof of holding a phone number, kept only as a keyed hash; one number per account. `VERIFY_PROVIDER` picks who sends the text: `off` (default; the badge is hidden), `dev` (the code is always `000000`; refused in production) or `vonage` (Vonage Verify v2 with `VONAGE_API_KEY`, `VONAGE_API_SECRET` and `VERIFY_BRAND`; the runbook is section 13 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-account-lifecycle-1b-design.md`, and `node scripts/verify-badge.mjs +4479xxxxxxxx` checks live credentials without touching an account). Routes: `POST /api/auth/badge/start`, `POST /api/auth/badge/verify`, `DELETE /api/auth/badge` (all fresh); starts are limited per account, per number, per IP and instance-wide (200 texts a day), and a flow takes at most five codes. The web build shows the badge section only with `WEB_BADGE=on` in `infra/.env` (the default is off, matching `API_VERIFY_PROVIDER=off`); set both together.
- **Data export.** `POST /api/account/export` (fresh, once a day) streams a ZIP with `export.json` (the account with its passkeys' names and dates, profile, posts, likes, reposts, bookmarks, following, blocks, mutes, sessions, devices and security events) and every image the account uploaded under `media/`. Nothing is kept on the server: the archive is written entry by entry (ZIP64 when it passes 4 GiB or 65,535 entries), `export.json` is read in pages and streamed last, image files go through the media module's checked open, a request that fails before the first byte gives the day's export back, and one that fails midway breaks the download instead of saving a truncated archive.
- **Deletion.** `POST /api/account/delete/start` (fresh) mails a code; `POST /api/account/delete/confirm` with the code and the handle typed by the owner deletes the account at once: every session is revoked, posts become tombstones with their images and any pending uploads deleted from disk, likes, reposts, bookmarks, follows, blocks and mutes go with their counters, the profile is anonymised and keeps its handle, and the auth row is anonymised. The `account-purge` job (every five minutes) finishes any deletion the process did not. Nothing can be undone.
## Passkeys and fresh authentication
An account can sign in and confirm sensitive changes with a passkey (WebAuthn), beside the emailed code, the password and two-factor codes. The API verifies through `@simplewebauthn/server`, which only `apps/api/src/auth/passkeys/webauthn.ts` imports; that module pins korbat's settings and adds its own checks.
- **Settings.** `API_WEBAUTHN_RP_ID` is the domain passkeys are bound to (default: the host of `API_APP_ORIGIN`) and `API_WEBAUTHN_RP_NAME` the name a browser shows (default `korbat`). Production refuses `localhost`, an IP address and anything that is not the origin's host or a parent domain of it. Browsers offer passkeys only on `https` or on `http://localhost`, never on an IP address.
- **Adding.** `POST /api/auth/passkeys/register/options` and `/verify`. Up to 10 passkeys, 5 added a day. User verification (fingerprint, face or PIN) is asked for and never required; only the attestation formats `none` and self-attested `packed` are accepted, so nothing is fetched from an authenticator's maker. The owner is mailed a link (`/passkey-remove/<token>`, valid 7 days, once) that removes the passkey and locks the account down.
- **Managing.** `GET /api/auth/passkeys` lists them (name, dates, synced, the device that added it, new, suspended); `PATCH /api/auth/passkeys/:id` renames and `DELETE` removes, the last one too. "This wasn't me" on a passkey of the last 7 days (`POST /api/auth/passkeys/:id/not-mine`) runs the lockdown.
- **Signing in.** `POST /api/auth/passkey/signin/options` and `/verify` need no email: the browser offers the passkeys it holds for the domain, from the button or from the email field's autofill. A sign-in whose passkey verified the user counts as two factors; one that did not is followed by the two-factor code on an account that has two-factor authentication, and staff who have it always give the code. A signature counter that goes backwards switches the passkey off, mails the owner and refuses the sign-in.
- **Fresh authentication has two levels.** A session records what it proved and when. Level 1 is any factor in the last five minutes; level 2 is a user-verified passkey, or a two-factor or recovery code beside an email code, a password or a passkey. An account with two-factor authentication needs level 2 to add or remove a passkey, change its password or email, set up, turn off or re-issue two-factor codes, and delete itself; every other account needs level 1. A refusal is `reauth_required` with `details: { level, have }`, and `POST /api/auth/reauth` (password, emailed code, two-factor code, recovery code or passkey) answers the level reached and rotates the session's token in place, so a copy of the old token stops working. Sessions are listed and revoked by an id that survives the rotation.
- **Lockdown.** Every "this wasn't me" runs one routine: the new-device alert's link, the passkey mail's remove link, the button on a new passkey, and the email change's undo. It signs out every session and device, removes the password, and removes the passkeys and two-factor authentication set up since the day before the event, in one transaction; the owner gets back in with a code sent to their email. A mailed link is tied to the address it went to: it does nothing while the account is at another address and works again once the address is restored.
- **What is kept.** The public key, the credential's id, its counter, whether it is synced and was registered with user verification, its name, and the device that added it; nothing about a fingerprint or a face ever leaves the person's device. The export lists names and dates only, and deleting the account deletes the passkeys.
- **After restoring a backup** the counters in the database are older than the authenticators': run `update auth.passkeys set counter = 0, suspect_at = null where suspect_at is null or suspect_at > '<restore time>';` so no passkey is refused as a copy (section 11 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-25-passkeys-1c-design.md`).
- **Tests.** A software authenticator (`apps/api/test/auth/soft-authenticator.ts`) runs whole ceremonies in the unit and integration suites. The API prints two `ExperimentalWarning`s at start, from the library's probe of Node 24's ML-DSA support; they are expected.

# korbat — Design Spec

## Context

A pseudonymous social network where people can post opinions without a platform quietly removing them. Content limits are narrow and public: no sexual content (+18), no CSAM, no targeted racial hatred or harassment, no doxxing, no direct threats, no spam. Everything else stays up. Opinions are never a removal reason.

The credibility of the product rests on two things, which is why they get half the effort each:

- **Security.** Users who post unpopular opinions are targets. Account takeover, deanonymization, brigading, and admin compromise all have to be designed against from day one.
- **Transparent moderation.** Every removal cites a public rule, shows the author exactly what happened, and can be appealed to a different human. There is no silent reach reduction and the feed is chronological, so "shadowban" is not something the system can do.

Decisions made during brainstorming:

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Platform | Expo (React Native) codebase, web target first, iOS/Android via EAS later |

## 3. Security design (threat by threat)

### 3a. Account takeover (own auth module, `apps/api/src/auth`)

The auth module is the single most reviewed code in the repo. It is one folder with one public interface, has no dependencies on the rest of the API, and every function in it has tests.

- **Sign-up**: email + 6-digit code (`auth.email_codes`, 10-minute expiry, 5 attempts, hashed at rest). Turnstile token verified server-side. Password optional at sign-up; if set, ≥ 12 chars and checked against the Have I Been Pwned range API (k-anonymity, only a 5-char SHA-1 prefix leaves the server). Hashed with argon2id (memory 64 MiB, iterations 3, parallelism 1; parameters stored alongside the hash for future upgrades).
- **Sign-in**: email code, or password. Constant-time comparison; identical response timing and message whether the email exists or not. After 5 failures the account locks for 15 minutes and the user gets an email.
- **MFA**: TOTP in v1 (`otplib`, secret encrypted with a server-side key from the secret store, 10 one-time recovery codes hashed). Passkeys in auth phase 2 with `@simplewebauthn/server`, then promoted as the default sign-in with password as fallback.
- **Sessions**: opaque 256-bit random ID; only its SHA-256 lives in `auth.sessions`. Web: httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie scoped to the API host, with a CSRF double-submit token for state-changing requests. Native: bearer token in SecureStore. Sliding 30-day expiry, 12-month absolute cap. Session ID is rotated on privilege changes (login, MFA enrol, password change).
- **Devices**: every session belongs to an `auth.devices` row. Users see their devices with platform, first/last seen, and can revoke one or all. First login from a new device sends an email alert with a one-click "this wasn't me" link that revokes it and forces a password reset.
- **Re-authentication**: handle change, email change, MFA removal, passkey removal, and account deletion require `reauth_at` within the last 5 minutes.
- **Rate limits**: per account, per email, and per IP/ASN sliding windows in Redis, with Cloudflare edge rules as the first layer on `/auth/*`.
- **Recovery**: email code to the verified address; if MFA is enabled, a recovery code is also required. No security questions, no SMS recovery (SIM-swap risk).
- **Explicit non-goals for v1**: OAuth social login (adds third-party trust and account-linking bugs), SMS login.

### 3b. Deanonymization and data leaks
- **PII minimization**: email and hashed phone exist only in `auth.users`. No API response type includes them except `/me`. Contracts in `packages/contracts` make this a compile-time property. The public API Postgres role has no SELECT on `auth.*`; the auth module uses its own role.
- **Images**: every upload is re-encoded with `sharp` (strips EXIF/GPS, defeats polyglot files), capped in pixel dimensions before decode (decompression-bomb guard), stored under a random ID, never the original filename. Uploads go through API-issued signed URLs with content-type and size constraints.
- **IP handling**: raw IPs never stored. Salted hash in `devices.ip_hash` for abuse correlation, purged by a 30-day job. Logs scrub tokens, emails, and IPs at the logger level.
- **No third-party trackers** in the client. Self-hosted, cookie-less analytics at most.
- **Defense in depth**: RLS enabled on every table even though only the API connects, so a SQL-injection or logic bug in one route cannot read another user's private rows. Three Postgres roles: `korbat_auth` (auth schema only), `korbat_api` (public schema, no DDL), `korbat_migrate` (DDL, used only by the migration job). MinIO bucket is private; media served through Cloudflare's CDN via short-lived signed URLs.
- **Encryption at rest**: Postgres and MinIO volumes on an encrypted disk (LUKS on the local server, provider-encrypted volume on the VPS). TOTP secrets additionally encrypted at the application layer. Backups encrypted with `age` before leaving the box.
- **User rights**: self-service data export (JSON) and account deletion (hard delete of profile/content, auth row anonymized) from v1.
- **Web hardening**: strict CSP with nonces, HSTS, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, CORS allowlist, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.

### 3c. Bots, spam, brigading, ban evasion
- Sign-up friction: email verification, Cloudflare Turnstile, disposable-email domain blocklist, per-IP/ASN sign-up limits.
- New accounts: for the first 24 hours, max 5 posts/hour and 20 replies/hour; no reposts or quotes for 48 hours. Limits relax as `reputation` grows (established accounts: 30 posts/hour).
- Reputation: a simple score (account age, verified badge, upheld reports, strikes). Used to weight reports and to size rate limits. Never used to rank or hide content.
- Report weighting: reports from low-reputation or brand-new accounts count less. A spike of reports on one target from accounts that follow each other is flagged as **brigading** and protects the target (case goes to a senior queue) rather than triggering action.
- Ban evasion: hashed device fingerprint + email-domain heuristics at sign-up; matches surface to moderators as a signal, not an auto-ban.
- Every write endpoint has a per-user and per-IP rate limit declared next to the route.

### 3d. Platform integrity
- Cloudflare WAF managed rules, DDoS protection, and edge rate limits before traffic reaches the API.
- Every request body validated with the shared Zod contract before any handler runs. Drizzle parameterizes all SQL.
- Admin console on its own hostname and deploy. Admin/moderator accounts require TOTP (passkey once available). Every action lands in the append-only `audit_log`.
- Secrets in Docker secrets / a root-only `.env` on the host, never in the repo. `.env.example` only. Containers run as non-root with read-only root filesystems where possible; only Postgres, MinIO, and Redis get writable volumes.
- **Backups**: nightly `pg_dump` + MinIO mirror, encrypted with `age`, pushed off-box (cheap object storage or a second machine). Restore is rehearsed in sub-project 4 and is also the migration path from VPS to the local server.
- **Home-server specifics**: Cloudflare Tunnel is the only ingress; SSH only over Tailscale or the LAN; automatic OS security updates; UPS or at least a clean-shutdown script so Postgres never sees power loss mid-write.
- CI: typecheck, vitest, semgrep (OWASP ruleset), `pnpm audit`, Renovate for dependency updates, OWASP ZAP baseline scan against a preview deploy.
- Image processing runs in a separate worker process with memory and time limits so a malicious file can't take down the API.
- Structured logging with request IDs; alerts on auth-failure spikes, 5xx rate, and moderation queue age.

| ID | Rule | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | No sexual or pornographic content | Automated image score → **withheld** pending human review. Human decides. |
| R2 | No child sexual abuse material | Cloudflare CSAM hash match → auto-remove, account suspended, NCMEC report. Only fully automated removal in the system. |
| R3 | No targeted hatred or harassment based on race, ethnicity, religion, or similar protected traits | Report-driven, human decision. Criticism of ideas, policies, groups' actions, or public figures is explicitly allowed. |
| R4 | No doxxing (publishing private info) | Report-driven, human. Withhold first when the report is credible, decide within SLA. |
| R5 | No direct threats of violence | Report-driven, human, senior queue. |
| R6 | No spam, scams, or impersonation | Report-driven, human. Reputation signals help triage. |

### What automation may and may not do
- **May**: score images for R1 and mark them `withheld`; hash-match for R2; raise priority on reports (e.g. many high-reputation reporters); flag brigading patterns.
- **May not**: remove text for any rule, reduce reach, hide from explore, or suspend an account (except R2).

### Case lifecycle
1. **Report** — reporter picks a rule, optionally adds context. Duplicate reports on the same target merge into one open case. Weight accumulates from reporter reputation.
2. **Queue** — moderators see cases sorted by weight and age. R2/R5 go to a senior queue. Brigading-flagged cases go to the senior queue with the target protected (no withholding on report volume alone).
3. **Decision** — moderator picks an action (`none`, `withhold`, `remove`, `strike`, `suspend`) and must write a `public_reason` citing the rule. The author sees the exact content that was actioned, the rule, the reason, and an **Appeal** button. Strikes expire after 90 days; suspension requires 3 active strikes or an R2/R5 case.
4. **Appeal** — one appeal per case, free-text statement. Reviewed by a different moderator. Outcome and reason are shown to the author. Upheld appeals reverse the action and remove the strike.
5. **Transparency** — every closed case is copied to `transparency_log` without user identifiers. Public `/transparency` page shows monthly counts per rule, per action, appeal rate, and reversal rate.

### What the author experiences
- A withheld post shows to the author as "Under review for R1, usually within 24h" with the content still visible to them. Nobody else sees it.
- A removed post stays in the author's view with a red status chip and the reason. It is never silently gone.
- Suspension shows the case list and a link to appeal each one.

## 6a. Importing posts from other platforms (added 2026-09-14)

Users can bring their post history with them. The feature is opt-in, owner-driven, and treated as untrusted bulk input.

**How a user imports**
1. The user downloads their own official data export from the other platform: X "Download an archive of your data", a Mastodon account archive, or a Bluesky repository export (`.car`). Meta (Threads/Instagram/Facebook) and Reddit exports come later as extra adapters.
2. They upload that file on korbat's import screen. korbat never asks for another platform's password, never scrapes, and does not fetch posts by handle. Fetching by handle would let anyone import someone else's posts, which is impersonation.
3. korbat parses the archive in the worker and shows a preview: post count, date range, image count, and what will be skipped. The user picks everything, a date range, and whether to include replies.
4. Nothing is published until the user confirms. The import can be undone later in one action, which deletes every post from that import.

**Security controls**
- **Quarantine**: archives upload through a short-lived signed URL into a separate private `korbat-imports` bucket, never the media bucket. Size cap 2 GB. The archive is deleted as soon as parsing finishes, or after 24 hours if the user abandons the preview.
- **Sandboxed parsing** in the worker with its own memory and time limits: streaming unzip, reject path traversal, absolute paths and symlinks, cap entry count and total uncompressed size (zip-bomb guard), and read only an allowlist of known file paths per source. X's `tweets.js` is stripped of its `window.YTD...=` prefix and parsed as JSON, never evaluated.
- **Data minimisation**: archives contain DMs, IP logs, phone numbers, contacts and ad data. Only the allowlisted post and media files are opened. Nothing else is read, stored or logged.
- **Same rules as native posts**: every imported image goes through the normal pipeline (re-encode, EXIF strip, NSFW score, CSAM hash). An R1 score withholds that post for human review. A CSAM match aborts the whole import and follows the R2 process. Text is never auto-removed; it can be reported like any post.
- **Anti-abuse**: email-verified accounts only, one active import per user, at most 50,000 posts per import, worker concurrency capped so imports cannot starve normal traffic.
- **Provenance label**: every imported post shows "Imported from X · original date". An optional ownership check (put a one-time korbat code in the source profile's bio) adds a "verified import" mark, which helps against people importing archives that are not theirs.

**Product behaviour**
- Imported posts appear on the author's profile in original date order. They do not enter followers' chronological feeds or explore, so a 10,000-post import cannot flood anyone's timeline.
- @mentions and links stay as plain text. korbat does not map other platforms' users to korbat accounts, so it never implies someone is on korbat.
- Reposts of other people's content are skipped. Threads of the user's own posts are kept as threads.

**Designed for expansion**
- Each source is an adapter with two functions: `detect(entries)` recognises the archive, and `readPosts(stream)` yields a normalised `ImportedPost` defined in `packages/contracts`. Adding Threads or Reddit is a new adapter; the pipeline, preview and moderation path do not change.
- Schema is added by migration in this sub-project: an `import_jobs` table (owner, source, status, stats, timestamps) and nullable columns on `posts` (`import_job_id`, `source`, `original_created_at`, `original_url`). These are additive, so the Foundation schema does not need to anticipate them.

Sensitive actions need a re-authentication within five minutes. 1b added profile, email, badge, export and deletion flows. The main design (`2026-09-03-korbat-design.md`, sections 3a and 6) names passkeys through SimpleWebAuthn as the promoted sign-in; `auth.passkeys` exists in the schema since the foundation and is unused.

The main risk for korbat's users is account takeover (they are pseudonymous and may be targeted). Passkeys remove phishing from sign-in, and a user-verified passkey is two factors in one gesture. They also add a way to keep access after a takeover: a passkey an attacker registers from a stolen session would survive every clean-up path that exists today (sign-out-everywhere, the not-me link, the email undo link). Sections 7.1, 7.2, 7.3 and 7.6 close that path; they also close a gap in 1b's email undo, where a password the attacker set before changing the address survived.

## 2. Decisions taken without the user

| Topic | Decision | Alternatives rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Passkeys are a first factor next to the email code and the password, with username-less sign-in (discoverable credentials) from a button and from the email field's autofill. Email stays the recovery root; removing the last passkey is allowed. | Passkeys as a second factor only; passkey-only accounts. |
| User verification | Options ask for `userVerification: "preferred"`; verification never requires it (`requireUserVerification: false`). A sign-in or re-authentication counts as two factors (`passkey_uv`) only when the assertion's UV flag is set and the passkey was registered with UV (`uv_initialized`); otherwise it is one factor (`passkey`). NIST SP 800-63B-4 ("SHALL indicate that UV is preferred ... treat as single-factor") and WebAuthn L3 section 7.2. | `required` everywhere (locks out security keys without a PIN). |
| TOTP after a passkey | On an account with TOTP, a `passkey` sign-in goes to the TOTP step and a `passkey_uv` one skips it, except for staff (role other than `user`), who always go to the TOTP step. A staff account without TOTP signs in as with an email code, and `requireStaffMfa` keeps refusing its staff routes. Whether a passkey satisfies staff MFA is sub-project 3's decision. | Letting a passkey satisfy staff MFA now. |
| Attestation | `attestationType: "none"`, and the API refuses any attestation format other than `none`, or `packed` without an `x5c` chain, before the library sees the response (`passkey_unsupported`). SimpleWebAuthn 14.0.2 fetches a certificate revocation list with no timeout or size limit for certificate-carrying formats, and the format comes from the client whatever was requested. | Trusting the library alone; MDS attestation. |
| Algorithms | `[-8, -7, -257]` (Ed25519, ES256, RS256), pinned on both the options and the verification call. ML-DSA (-48) stays out while Node marks it experimental. | The library's runtime default, which adds ML-DSA on Node 24. |
| Challenges | Stateless: 58 bytes carrying purpose, expiry, a nonce and, for registration and re-authentication, a hash of the session's handle, sealed with an HMAC keyed from `AUTH_HMAC_KEY`; valid 10 minutes (the top of WebAuthn L3's recommended range); checked by korbat before the library runs, then made single-use with a `setIfAbsent` marker. Handing out options stores nothing, so the unauthenticated sign-in options endpoint cannot fill Valkey (which runs with `noeviction`) beyond its per-IP rate counters. This departs from the main design's "challenges in Redis". | Challenges stored in Valkey at options time. |
| User handle | A random 32-byte `users.webauthn_user_id`, set once with a compare-and-set; never the account id. | The account uuid. |
| Name in the password manager | `user.name` and `user.displayName` are a label the owner types when adding a passkey, prefilled with the handle; the dialog says the password manager shows it to anyone who can see the device. korbat does not store it. | Always the handle; always the email. |
| Per-account bounds | 10 passkeys at a time, and 5 added in any 24 hours (counted from `passkey_added` events, so removing a passkey does not free a slot of the day). No source gives a number; flagged. | Unlimited. |
| Session factors | Every session records which factors it has proven and when (`sessions.factors`: kind to epoch milliseconds; kinds `email`, `password`, `totp`, `recovery`, `passkey`, `passkey_uv`). Sign-in writes the factors of the sign-in, and each re-authentication adds one. `reauth_at` is always the latest of them. "Fresh" keeps its meaning: any factor within five minutes. "Fresh at level 2" means, within those five minutes, `passkey_uv`, or `totp` or `recovery` together with `email`, `password` or `passkey`. | A stored level number (it went stale). |
| When level 2 is needed | Only when the account has TOTP (the session carries `mfaEnabled`). Then these need level 2: adding and removing a passkey, TOTP setup and disable, regenerating recovery codes, setting or changing the password, starting an email change, and starting or confirming account deletion. An account without TOTP stays at today's level 1. TOTP brings recovery codes, and re-authentication accepts a recovery code (section 7.2), so an owner who loses the TOTP device can still reach level 2 inside a session. | Level 2 whenever any second factor exists (an account whose only UV passkey was lost could never reach level 2 again). |
| Token rotation | `sessions.rotate` replaces the token in place for every caller (re-authentication, password set, TOTP confirm, handle choice): only the row's id, the token's hash, changes; its handle, factors, `reauth_at`, `passkey_id`, device and expiries stay; the old id gets a tombstone. A re-authentication therefore rotates as ASVS 5.0 7.2.4 asks, and an owner's re-authentication kills a thief's copy. | Keeping the token on re-authentication (1a); a new row per rotation (loses the factors). |
| Session handle | `sessions.handle`, a random uuid that rotation keeps. The re-authentication limits and the challenge binding use it, so rotation cannot reset them and no other session of the account can spend them. | Keying them by account (any session could lock the owner out). |
| TOTP setup, confirm and disable | Setup needs level 2 on an account that already has TOTP (it replaces the secret and the recovery codes). Confirm needs only the pending secret that setup stored for this session's handle, so scanning the QR code cannot run the window out. Disable needs level 2 and no longer takes a code (the TOTP re-authentication has already spent the current step). | Asking for a second code 30 seconds later. |
| Lockdown | One routine, used by the not-me link, the passkey remove link, the email undo link and the Security screen's "not mine": every session revoked with tombstones, every device revoked, the password cleared, passkeys created at or after `since` removed (plus any passkey the caller names), and TOTP turned off when it was enabled at or after `since`. `since` is 24 hours before the caller's anchor: the alerted sign-in (not-me), the passkey's creation (remove link, "not mine"), or the address change (email undo). A TOTP secret or password an attacker set just before the event is caught whichever link the owner uses. | Device-provenance rules (they miss an attacker who uses two devices); an anchor with no look-back. |
| Links tied to their address | Every link 1c mails (not-me, passkey remove) stores the keyed hash of the address it was sent to; it does nothing when the account's address has changed since. Links mailed to an attacker's address die when the email undo restores the owner's. | Links valid whatever happens to the address. |
| Notification | Adding a passkey mails the account with a one-shot remove link, valid 7 days, which runs the lockdown. Removing one mails the account too. The Security screen marks passkeys added in the last 7 days as new and offers "not mine" on them. | Mail without a link. |
| Counter | The library is given a stored counter of 0, so it never decides on counters. A regression is `(new > 0 or stored > 0) and new <= stored` (WebAuthn L3 section 6.1.1); two zeros, as Apple passkeys always send, are not. After the signature is verified, korbat writes the counter with one conditional update; when the update matches no row, the passkey is marked `suspect_at`, `passkey_counter_regression` is recorded and mailed, and the attempt is refused. A suspect passkey is refused from then on (`passkey_suspended`, answered only after its signature checked) until the owner removes it. The restore runbook resets counters. | Letting the library throw before it checks the signature (an unsigned response could mark a passkey). |
| Signal API | None in 1c. WebKit bugs 322267 and 302624 (open) make the Signal API delete other accounts' passkeys on Safari, and `signalUnknownCredential` is in the affected family. | `signalUnknownCredential` on unknown credentials. |
| Browser library | `@simplewebauthn/browser` 14.0.0 (no dependencies): base64url handling, conditional UI with its abort service, typed errors, feature detection. | Calling `navigator.credentials` with the native JSON helpers (Chrome 129, Firefox 119, Safari 18.4 only). |
| Automatic passkey upgrade | Not in 1c (Chrome's conditional create). | - |
| Native | Nothing native in 1c; the RP ID is configurable so sub-project 5 can share it through associated domains and `assetlinks.json`. | - |

## 3. Research (facts the design depends on)

- **@simplewebauthn/server 14.0.2** (MIT, published 2026-09-13, 12 days old on 2026-09-25) is the only version that fixes GHSA-j3h4-m3m2-7p7j (blind SSRF through the CRL URL of an attacker's x5c) and GHSA-2g3p-m8c9-hhwh (CRL cache poisoning); neither has a CVE and scanners do not list them, so `pnpm audit` would not flag an older pin. Dependencies: 24 packages, 3.2 MB, all MIT, BSD-3-Clause (asn1js), Apache-2.0 (reflect-metadata) or 0BSD (tslib); no install scripts; no trust downgrade. `@peculiar/asn1-*` 2.9.5 is under the 7-day floor until 2026-09-27T18:16Z, so a floored install resolves 2.9.4. `reflect-metadata` modifies the global `Reflect` object at import. On Node 24.21 (the API image) the package detects ML-DSA at import and prints two `ExperimentalWarning`s on every start. It declares `node >= 20`; its npm build is tested only under Deno in its CI.
- **@simplewebauthn/browser 14.0.0** (MIT, 2026-09-02) has no dependencies.
- `@simplewebauthn/server/helpers` exports `decodeAttestationObject`, `decodeClientDataJSON`, `isoCBOR`, `isoBase64URL` and `parseAuthenticatorData`: korbat's own checks and the tests' software authenticator use them.
- `verifyAuthenticationResponse` looks at nothing but the credential it is given: it does not check the user handle or which account the credential belongs to. It throws on a counter regression before it verifies the signature. `verifyRegistrationResponse` never compares `response.id` with the authenticator data's credential id (korbat stores `registrationInfo.credential.id`), takes `transports` from the client unchecked, and has no 1023-byte credential-id limit. `generateRegistrationOptions` writes into the `authenticatorSelection` object it is given.
- Conditional mediation ignores the ceremony timeout, so an autofill request can outlive its challenge.
- WebAuthn refuses an origin whose host is an IP address. The built-in browser used for walk-throughs has no virtual authenticator.
- Apple passkeys always report a signature counter of 0.
- Both passkey Permissions-Policy features default to `self`; the Caddyfile needs no change.
- No table references `auth.sessions.id`, so its value can change in place.

## 5. Architecture

A folder `apps/api/src/auth/passkeys/` inside the auth module (passkeys are credentials and stay in the auth schema):

- `challenge.ts`: `issueChallenge(key, purpose, now, bind?)`, `readChallenge(key, challenge, purpose, now, bind?)` and `spendChallenge(kv, challenge, now)`. The 58 bytes: version (1), purpose (1: sign-in 1, registration 2, re-authentication 3), expiry in ms (8, big-endian), nonce (16 random), binding (16: the first 16 bytes of `sha256(sessionHandle)`, zeros for sign-in), MAC (16: HMAC-SHA256 truncated) over the first 42. The key is `HKDF-SHA256(AUTH_HMAC_KEY, "korbat-webauthn-challenge")`. `readChallenge` checks the MAC in constant time, then purpose, binding and expiry (`challenge_expired`); `spendChallenge` writes `webauthn-used:<sha256(challenge)>` with `setIfAbsent` for the remaining lifetime and refuses a second use (`passkey_invalid`).
- `webauthn.ts`: the only file that imports `@simplewebauthn/server`. Options: a fresh `authenticatorSelection` object on every call (`residentKey: "required"`, `userVerification: "preferred"`), the pinned algorithms, `timeout: 300000`, `attestationType: "none"`. Verification, in this order: decode `clientDataJSON` and run `readChallenge` and `spendChallenge` on its challenge (a spent challenge stays spent whatever happens next); for registration, the format check (`decodeAttestationObject`: `fmt` is `none`, or `packed` with no `x5c`), then the library with `requireUserVerification: false`, the expected origins, the RP ID, `expectedChallenge` set to the checked string and `supportedAlgorithmIDs` pinned, then the credential id (at most 1023 bytes) and `transports` (known values only, at most 5) from `registrationInfo`; for authentication, the library with the stored credential and a counter of 0. Any library error becomes `passkey_invalid`.
- `flows.ts`: `registerOptions`, `registerVerify`, `listPasskeys`, `renamePasskey`, `removePasskey`, `notMine`, `removeByLink`, `signInOptions`, `signInVerify`, `reauthOptions`, and the `passkey` and `recovery` methods of the re-authentication flow.
- `apps/api/src/auth/flows/lockdown.ts`: the lockdown routine.
- The auth repository gains the passkey rows, the user handle, the conditional counter update, the factor writes and the in-place rotation; `signIn` gains an options argument `{ factors, passkeyId, method }` and records `metadata.method` on `signin_success` for every method. The account module receives both freshness middlewares and grows by what deletion and export need.

## 6. Data model (migration 0010)

- `auth.users.webauthn_user_id bytea unique` (null until the first registration; set with `update ... set webauthn_user_id = coalesce(webauthn_user_id, $new) returning webauthn_user_id`).
- `auth.passkeys` keeps `id`, `user_id`, `credential_id`, `public_key`, `counter`, `transports`, `backed_up`, `name`, `created_at`, `last_used_at`, and gains `aaguid uuid`, `backup_eligible boolean not null default false`, `uv_initialized boolean not null default false`, `created_device_id uuid null references auth.devices on delete set null`, `suspect_at timestamptz null`; a unique index on `credential_id` if it is not there. `created_at` is written from the flows' clock, like `users.password_changed_at` and `totp_enabled_at`, because the lockdown compares them with flow times.
- `auth.sessions` gains `handle uuid not null default gen_random_uuid()`, `factors jsonb not null default '{}'` and `passkey_id uuid null`. Sessions that exist when the migration runs have no factors and re-authenticate before their next sensitive action.
- Event types: `passkey_added`, `passkey_removed`, `passkey_counter_regression`. Sign-ins keep `signin_success`, now with `metadata.method` (`email`, `password`, `passkey`).
- Mail templates: `passkey_added` (with the remove link), `passkey_removed`, `passkey_suspect`; `new_device`, `email_changed` and `email_change_reverted` describe the lockdown.

## 7. Flows

### 7.1 Session factors and freshness

Sign-in writes the session's factors: `email` for an email code, `password` for a password, `passkey` or `passkey_uv` for a passkey; the MFA step adds `totp` or `recovery`; the MFA ticket carries `{ userId, deviceId, platform, factors, passkeyId, method }`. `sessions.rotate` is the in-place rotation and does not touch `reauth_at`: freshness moves only when a factor is written. TOTP confirm writes `totp` (its code proves the new secret) and calls `forgetUser` before rotating, so the cached `mfaEnabled` is right. The auth module exports `requireFreshAuth` (level 1) and `requireStrongFreshAuth` (level 2 when the account has TOTP, level 1 otherwise); both read `factors`. A refusal is 403 `reauth_required` with `details: { level, have }` (`have`: the factor kinds within the window), and the error envelope and the app's `ApiError` gain an optional `details` object.

### 7.2 Re-authentication

`POST /api/auth/reauth` accepts `password`, `totp`, `email` (as today), `recovery` and `passkey`:

- `recovery` `{ recoveryCode }` needs an account with TOTP (`mfa_required` otherwise), consumes the code with `consumeRecoveryCodeHash`, records `recovery_code_used` with the session's device and mails the owner; a wrong code is `invalid_mfa_code` and records `signin_failure` with `metadata.method = "recovery_code"`. The answer adds `mustReenrollMfa: true`.
- `passkey` `{ response }` follows `POST /api/auth/reauth/passkey/options` (options with the account's usable passkeys in `allowCredentials` and a challenge bound to the session's handle). The passkey is looked up by credential id **and** the session's user (`where credential_id = $1 and user_id = $2`): another account's passkey and an unknown one are `passkey_invalid`, and the challenge is spent. After the signature checks, a suspect passkey is `passkey_suspended`, and the counter rule applies as at sign-in; on success `counter`, `last_used_at` and `backed_up` are written.

Every method writes its factor and `reauth_at`, rotates the token in place, and answers `{ level, token?, mustReenrollMfa? }`, with the new token in the body for a bearer session and in the cookies for a web session. Limits: `reauth:<handle>` 5 per 15 minutes counted at each verification, `reauth-email:<handle>` 3 an hour, and `reauth:ip:<ipHash>` 30 per 15 minutes; wrong passwords keep counting towards the account lockout as today.

### 7.3 Registration

`POST /api/auth/passkeys/register/options` `{ label }` (session, onboarding, strong-fresh): `passkey_limit` (409, `details: { reason: "cap" }`) at 10 passkeys, or (`details: { reason: "daily", retryAfter }`) at 5 `passkey_added` events in the last 24 hours; sets `webauthn_user_id` when missing; answers `{ options }` with `excludeCredentials` listing the account's passkeys, `user.name` and `displayName` set to the label, and a challenge bound to the session's handle. Limit `passkey-register:<handle>` 10 an hour.

`POST /api/auth/passkeys/register/verify` `{ name?, response }` (session, onboarding; the challenge, issued only to a strong-fresh session and bound to its handle, stands in for a second freshness check, so a ceremony that outlasts the window still completes): the checks of section 5; then one transaction locks the user row `for update`, checks both bounds again, inserts the passkey (flags, AAGUID, `created_device_id`, `created_at`, name, default "Passkey on <platform>") and inserts the `passkey_added` event in the same transaction; the session's factors do not change; the owner gets `passkey_added` with a remove link (`passkey-remove:<sha256(token)>` in Valkey for 7 days, holding `{ userId, passkeyId, createdAt, sentTo }`). Answers `{ passkey }`. When this step fails after the browser created the passkey, the app says the password manager may have kept a passkey korbat does not know and can be told to delete it there.

### 7.4 Sign-in

`POST /api/auth/passkey/signin/options` (no session): `{ options }` with an empty `allowCredentials` and a sign-in challenge. Limit 30 per IP per 15 minutes.

`POST /api/auth/passkey/signin/verify` `{ response }` plus the device fields and the web/native handling exactly as `POST /api/auth/password/signin` takes them (no session): the passkey by credential id (unknown, or its account deleted: `passkey_invalid`); the response's user handle, when present, must equal the account's `webauthn_user_id`; the library; then a suspect passkey is `passkey_suspended`; then the counter rule's conditional update (which also writes `last_used_at` and `backed_up`). When no device of the account matches the request's device, `passkey-signin-new-device:<userId>` counts 5 an hour and 20 a day (a failed sign-in is never counted, so nobody but the passkey's holder can spend it). Then: when the account has TOTP and the sign-in is `passkey` or the account is staff, an MFA ticket is written and `mfa_required` answered; otherwise `signIn` is called directly with `{ factors, passkeyId, method: "passkey" }`. The new-device alert works as for the other methods. Limit 20 per IP per 15 minutes; no account lockout.

### 7.5 Management

`GET /api/auth/passkeys` (session): `{ passkeys: PasskeyView[] }` (id, name, created, last used, synced, the creating device's label, whether it started this session, `new` for the last 7 days, `suspended`). `PATCH /api/auth/passkeys/:id` `{ name }` (session) and `DELETE /api/auth/passkeys/:id` (session, strong-fresh) act only on a passkey of the session's account (`where id = $1 and user_id = $2`; otherwise 404 `passkey_not_found`); removal records `passkey_removed` and mails the account; the last passkey can go. `POST /api/auth/passkeys/:id/not-mine` (session, fresh at level 1, only for a passkey of the account created in the last 7 days, 5 per account a day) runs the lockdown anchored at that passkey's creation and names it; level 1 is enough because an attacker who re-enrolled TOTP holds level 2. `GET /api/me` gains `user.passkeys` (the count).

### 7.6 Lockdown and the links

- **not-me**: `NotMeToken` becomes `{ userId, deviceId, signedInAt, passkeyId?, sentTo }`, written by every sign-in's alert (`signedInAt` from the flows' clock). The lockdown is anchored at `signedInAt` and names `passkeyId`. A token from before the deploy (no `signedInAt`, or the bare-user-id form) is anchored at the moment it is used minus its 24-hour lifetime, so it removes more, never less.
- **Passkey remove link** `POST /api/auth/passkeys/remove-link` `{ token }` (no session, 10 per IP an hour, the token taken once): anchored at the stored `createdAt`, so it works even when the passkey is already gone. Page `/passkey-remove/<token>`.
- **Email undo** (1b): anchored at the address change; the revert state also records the changing session's `passkey_id`, which the lockdown names. A passkey created earlier than 24 hours before the change, and not the one that made the change, is left to its own remove link (7 days) and to the new-device alert of its next sign-in: the lockdown revokes every device, so that alert fires and its not-me link names the passkey.
- Every link checks `sentTo` against the keyed hash of the account's current address and does nothing (`invalid_code`, "This link has expired or was already used.") when they differ or the account is gone.
- Every lockdown records its caller's event with the removed passkeys and whether TOTP and the password were cleared; the mails and pages that offer these links say what the lockdown does.

### 7.7 Deletion and export

`takeDown` deletes the passkeys and clears `webauthn_user_id` in its transaction; `finishTakeDown` repeats it. The export's `account.passkeys` lists `{ name, createdAt, lastUsedAt, synced, createdOn }` (no keys, no ids; the format name stays `korbat-export-1`; the field is additive).

## 8. Contracts

`PasskeyName` and `PasskeyLabel` (the display-name rules), `PasskeyView`, `PasskeyListResponse`, `PasskeyRegisterOptionsRequest`, `PasskeyOptionsResponse` (the options JSON, validated loosely), `PasskeyRegisterVerifyRequest`, `PasskeySignInVerifyRequest`, `PasskeyRenameRequest`, `PasskeyRemoveLinkRequest`, the `recovery` and `passkey` re-authentication methods, `ReauthResponse { level, token?, mustReenrollMfa? }`, `MeResponse.user.passkeys`, the error envelope's optional `details`, and the response JSON shapes bounded in size (credential id at most 1400 base64url characters, `clientDataJSON` 4 KiB, `attestationObject` 64 KiB, `authenticatorData` 4 KiB, `signature` 1 KiB, `userHandle` 128 characters). The TOTP disable request becomes an empty body. New error codes: `passkey_invalid`, `passkey_unsupported`, `passkey_limit`, `passkey_suspended`, `passkey_not_found`, `challenge_expired`.

- **Messages**: `passkey_invalid` "That passkey did not work. Try again or use your email.", `passkey_unsupported` "This passkey provider is not supported. Try another one.", `passkey_limit` "You have 10 passkeys. Remove one to add another." or, for the daily bound, "You added 5 passkeys today. ## 10. Security

Rate limits (429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After`):

| Endpoint | Guards | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| sign-in options | none | 30 per IP per 15 min |
| sign-in verify | none | 20 per IP per 15 min; from a new device, 5 per account an hour and 20 a day |
| register options | session, onboarding, strong-fresh | `passkey-register:<handle>` 10 an hour |
| register verify | session, onboarding; bound challenge | - |
| re-auth (every method) | session | `reauth:<handle>` 5 per 15 min, `reauth-email:<handle>` 3 an hour, `reauth:ip:<ipHash>` 30 per 15 min |
| rename | session | 30 per account an hour |
| remove | session, strong-fresh | 10 per account an hour |
| not mine | session, fresh | 5 per account a day |
| remove link | none | 10 per IP an hour |

Level 2 (strong-fresh when the account has TOTP): `POST /api/auth/passkeys/register/options`, `DELETE /api/auth/passkeys/:id`, `POST /api/auth/mfa/totp/setup`, `/mfa/totp/disable`, `/mfa/recovery-codes/regenerate`, `/password/set`, `/email/change/start`, `/api/account/delete/start` and `/delete/confirm`. Level 1 as today: `/email/change/verify`, `/api/account/export`, the badge routes, session revocation. `/mfa/totp/confirm` drops its freshness check: it needs the session and the pending secret its setup stored for the session's handle.

Other bounds: 10 passkeys and 5 added a day (`passkey_limit`, 409); every challenge single-use; every link taken once and tied to its address.

## 5. Architecture

The auth module owns staff keys, staff sessions and enrolments (schema `auth`, role `korbat_auth`), because they are credentials. It gains a staff part (`apps/api/src/auth/staff/`): the enrolment flow, the staff sign-in flow, the staff session store and middleware (`requireStaff`, `requireStaffStepUp(action, target)`), and the step-up flow. All run through the existing WebAuthn wrapper and 1c's challenge codec, with the console's relying party ID and origin. The codec and the wrapper raise 1c's `passkey_invalid`, and the wrapper also `passkey_unsupported`; the staff flows catch both and raise a staff code in their place (section 10), so no staff answer carries a `passkey_*` code. The codec gains two purposes in its 58-byte layout, `staff_signin` (no binding) and `staff_register` (bound to the enrolment's id); its key, 10-minute lifetime and used-challenge markers stay as they are, and its binding argument becomes any string rather than only a session's handle. Step-up challenges (`staff_step_up`, bound to the staff session's handle and the action's hash) get a layout of their own in the same codec, with the same key, lifetime and markers: 74 bytes of version, purpose, expiry, nonce, the full 32-byte binding and the 16-byte seal, so `audit-verify` can recompute the binding from a stored assertion without the sealing key (section 7) and the console can check it before it asks for the key (section 9); the layout's offsets and the purpose's code are in the contracts, which the codec, `audit-verify` and the console read. The wrapper's `registrationOptions` and `authenticationOptions` gain a `userVerification` argument: `"preferred"` for 1c's callers and `"required"` for every staff ceremony. `registrationOptions` also gains an optional security-key hint, passed to the library as `preferredAuthenticatorType: "securityKey"`, and `verifyAuthentication` also reports the BE flag. Verification keeps `requireUserVerification: false`. The staff flows refuse on the reported flags after the library has verified, so each refusal has its own code.

A new module, `apps/api/src/staff/` (role `korbat_api`), owns the audit reader, `chain.ts` (the canonical text in Node, section 6) and the writer for actions on public tables (3c is its first user; the auth module writes auth-side rows through `auth.append_audit`), and mounts the staff routes at `/api/staff/*`, using the auth module's interface as the other modules do (`createAuth` exposes `requireStaff`, `requireStaffStepUp` and the staff flows). `createApp` mounts the staff routes with the other modules, after the global rate limit, and only while `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` is set. It passes the app's CORS and `resolveSession` over every path that is `/api/staff` or begins with `/api/staff/`, with one path test, as it already passes the upload route over the JSON body limit (`apps/api/src/app.ts:48`). The staff routes so keep the request id and access log, the client identity, the API's security headers and `no-store`, the 64 KiB body limit and the global rate limit, and never meet the app's CORS, its Origin allow-list, its session or its CSRF check: the staff gate and `requireStaff` (section 7) are their only origin and session logic. Without the skip, `resolveSession` would answer every console POST 403 `csrf_failed`, since its allow-list is `APP_ORIGIN` and `TRUSTED_ORIGINS` (`apps/api/src/auth/middleware.ts:20-24`, `apps/api/src/auth/context.ts:40`), and the app's credentialed CORS (`app.ts:43`) would answer staff reads for every trusted origin. Listing the console in `TRUSTED_ORIGINS` is not a way round it, and the configuration refuses it (section 8).

The auth module keeps the credential parts of the staff routes. It raises every staff error code as an `AuthError` (section 10). Its index exports `setStaffCookies` and `clearStaffCookies` (`auth/staff/cookies.ts`), which the staff module's sign-in and sign-out routes call, as it already exports `clearSessionCookies` to the account module. The staff cookies' names and attributes stay the auth module's, and `requireStaff` is their only reader. The staff module raises no code of its own; a malformed request is 400 `invalid_request`, as elsewhere. The architecture test's module list gains `staff`, with two rules of its own. First, inside `staff/`, no auth-schema table or enum is imported from `@korbat/db`, as for the social and media modules. Second, neither `createAuthRepo` nor `createSessions` is imported from the auth module. `cli/staff.ts` joins `config.ts`, `index.ts` and `cli/grant-role.ts` in `AUTH_POOL_ALLOWED`, since it opens both pools as grant-role does. Each new rule has a test that catches a deliberate violation, as the existing rules have. The command line `apps/api/src/cli/staff.ts` (built as `dist/staff.js` beside `dist/grant-role.js`, from an entry in `apps/api/build.mjs`) enrols (run in the migrate service, as the migrator), lists and revokes keys, reads the audit head and verifies the record. The console is `apps/admin`: React, esbuild and CSS, built into Caddy's image like the app's export and served by Caddy's second site.

## 6. Data model (migration 0012)

- `auth.staff_keys`: `id uuid` primary key, `user_id` references `auth.users` on delete restrict (below), `credential_id text` unique, `public_key text`, `counter bigint`, `transports text[]`, `name text`, `aaguid uuid null`, `origin text` and `rp_id text` (the console's origin and relying party ID the key was enrolled for, copied from its enrolment), `created_at`, `last_used_at null`, `revoked_at null`, `suspect_at null` (set together with `revoked_at` when a counter goes backwards, section 7: a suspect staff key is a revoked key whose record says why), and a unique (`id`, `user_id`) that the staff sessions' foreign key names. A key is live while `revoked_at` is null; `suspect_at` is never set without it. Only keys whose registration reported UV and no BE exist; the flow refuses the others before the insert. A key has no user handle of its own: staff registrations use the account's `auth.users.webauthn_user_id` (1c's random 32 bytes, never the account id). A staff key row is never deleted: revocation and an account's take-down set `revoked_at`, because `audit-verify` checks old confirmations against its public key, owner, origin and relying party ID. The migration revokes what 0001's default privileges grant, as 0003 does. `korbat_auth` keeps SELECT and UPDATE of `counter`, `last_used_at`, `revoked_at` and `suspect_at` only, and a `before update` trigger refuses to clear or change a set `revoked_at` or `suspect_at`. Keys are inserted only by `auth.add_staff_key(token, credential_id, public_key, counter, transports, name, aaguid, request_id)`. This is a migrator-owned `security definer` function with its `search_path` pinned to `auth, pg_temp` (`public.audit_log` named in full), EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC and granted to `korbat_auth`. It hashes the token itself, so a token hash read from the table spends nothing. In the caller's transaction it spends the matching enrolment only if the enrolment is unused and unexpired. It checks, under a `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` lock on the account's row (section 7), that the account is live and staff, has a handle, and holds fewer than five live keys. It then inserts the key, with the origin and relying party ID its enrolment recorded, and writes its `staff_key_added` audit row, which names the key in `key_id` and as its target (`target_type` `staff_key`). `auth.forget_staff_keys(user_id)` is a migrator-owned function of the same kind (`security definer`, the same `search_path`, EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC and granted to `korbat_auth`). For an account marked deleted it sets every staff key's `name` to an empty string, `aaguid` to null and `transports` to an empty array; for a live account it changes nothing. The take-down calls it (section 7), so a deleted account's keys keep no name, AAGUID or transports: only what `audit-verify` needs (the id, the owner, the credential id, the public key, the origin and the relying party ID), their counter and their dates.
- `auth.staff_enrollments`: `id uuid`, `user_id` references `auth.users` on delete cascade, `token_hash text` unique, `origin text` and `rp_id text` (`CONSOLE_ORIGIN` and the console's relying party ID when `staff.js enroll` issued it), `created_at`, `expires_at`, `used_at null`. The link's token is 32 random bytes; only its sha256 is kept. `korbat_auth` holds SELECT and DELETE only: it cancels an open enrolment by deleting it (a role change, `revoke ... all`, the take-down), and the janitor deletes spent ones. The same never-cleared trigger, on this table, refuses to clear a set `used_at`. Enrolments are inserted only by `staff.js enroll` as the migrator and spent only by `auth.add_staff_key`.
- `auth.staff_sessions`: `id text` primary key (the token's sha256), `handle uuid` unique, `user_id` references `auth.users` on delete cascade, `key_id`, with (`key_id`, `user_id`) referencing `auth.staff_keys (id, user_id)` on delete cascade, so a session's key always belongs to its account whoever writes the row, `created_at`, `last_used_at` (the time of the person's last action, section 7), `idle_expires_at`, `absolute_expires_at`, `revoked_at null`, `browser text` (the coarse label that `deviceLabel(userAgent, "web")` gives (`apps/api/src/auth/devices.ts:7`), such as "Firefox on Linux"; shown in the session list and the sign-in mail; never the user agent itself, never an IP). A check keeps `absolute_expires_at` at most 12 hours after `created_at` and `idle_expires_at` at most `absolute_expires_at`. `requireStaff` refuses a session whose `created_at` is later than now. A session row written behind the flows' back therefore ends within 12 hours.
- Accounts are never deleted by korbat, only marked deleted and anonymised (`apps/api/src/auth/repo.ts:226-284`), so the `on delete cascade` of `user_id` never fires, and staff keys are never deleted, so neither does the sessions' cascade from their key; the take-down and the janitor act on staff rows by name (section 7, Role changes, deletion and retention). The development cleaner does delete test accounts, as the superuser (`scripts/clean-dev-fixtures.mjs:112`); `staff_keys.user_id` is therefore `on delete restrict` and the cleaner skips an account that holds a staff key, so no delete takes a key with it and `audit-verify` keeps every public key it needs.
- `auth.auth_event_type` gains `staff_enrollment_issued`, `staff_key_added`, `staff_key_revoked`, `staff_key_counter_regression`, `staff_sign_in`, `staff_signed_out`, `staff_session_revoked`.
- `public.audit_log` gains `staff_session uuid null` (the session's handle), `key_id uuid null`, `action_hash text null`, `step_up jsonb null` (the assertion's `authenticatorData`, `clientDataJSON` and `signature`, base64url exactly as the browser sent them), with a check that `action_hash` and `step_up` are both null or both set and that `key_id` is set when they are, and four chain columns, each NOT NULL with a placeholder default that the trigger always overwrites: `chain_version smallint default 0`, `chain_seq bigint default 0`, `prev_hash text default ''`, `hash text default ''`. The placeholders keep drizzle's insert type from requiring them (the staff module's writer supplies none of them) and let `ADD COLUMN` succeed over the rows the development and test databases already hold. A unique index on `chain_seq` serves the viewer and `audit-verify`; there is no `(created_at desc, id desc)` index.
- `public.audit_head (id smallint primary key check (id = 1), seq bigint not null, hash text not null)` holds one row: the place and hash of the newest row, `(1, 0, 64 zeros)` for an empty log. It is declared in `safety.ts` with RLS and one policy, `audit_head_api_select`, FOR SELECT TO `korbat_api`. The migration revokes everything the default privileges of `0001_grants.sql:16` give `korbat_api` on it and grants SELECT back, so `korbat_api` reads the head and can neither write it nor lock it (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE needs UPDATE). On `audit_log` it keeps INSERT and SELECT; UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE stay revoked (`0001_grants.sql:20`).
- The trigger `audit_chain`, BEFORE INSERT FOR EACH ROW, runs `public.audit_chain()`: SECURITY DEFINER, owned by `korbat_migrate`, created with `SET search_path = public, pg_temp` as `0007` pins `auth, pg_temp`, with every object named schema-qualified, so no temporary table can stand in for the head; EXECUTE on it is revoked from PUBLIC (the trigger still fires for `korbat_api`). It locks the head row with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE and raises `the audit head is missing` when there is none, never creating one. It then sets, ignoring whatever the insert supplied: `id` to `nextval('public.audit_log_id_seq')`, so an insert naming an existing id with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` cannot move the head without writing its row; `created_at` to `clock_timestamp()` truncated to milliseconds, the time the row joined the chain; `chain_version` to 1; `chain_seq` to the head's `seq` + 1; `prev_hash` to the head's hash; and `hash` to the lower-case hex sha256 of the UTF-8 bytes of `prev_hash` followed by the row's canonical text (`encode(sha256(convert_to(..., 'UTF8')), 'hex')`, never a cast to bytea, which reads backslashes as escapes). Last it moves the head to the row's `chain_seq` and `hash`. The chain's order is `chain_seq`, the order in which inserts took the head's lock; it has no gaps, since a rolled-back insert rolls the head back with it. The lock lasts until the inserting transaction ends (section 7).
- **The canonical text, version 1**, is defined here and nowhere else, and implemented twice: in SQL by `public.audit_chain_text(r public.audit_log)` (plpgsql, the same `search_path`, EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC), which the trigger and the back-fill call; and in Node by the staff module's `chain.ts`, which `audit-verify` calls and which never asks the database to compute a text or a hash, since the owner can replace the database's functions. It is a JSON array with no whitespace between its elements, in this order: `chain_version`, `chain_seq` and `id` as decimal integers; `created_at` in UTC as a JSON string of the form `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ` (in SQL `to_char(created_at at time zone 'UTC', 'YYYY-MM-DD"T"HH24:MI:SS.MS"Z"')`, in Node `toISOString()`); `actor_id`, `action`, `target_type`, `target_id`, `request_id`, `staff_session`, `key_id` and `action_hash`, each a JSON string (uuids in PostgreSQL's lower-case form) or `null` when the column is null; then `step_up` and last `metadata`, each exactly as PostgreSQL prints the stored jsonb (`::text`: jsonb's own key order, `", "` and `": "` separators), or `null` when `step_up` is null, which the verifier reads with that cast and never re-serialises. Strings are escaped as `to_json(text)` and `JSON.stringify` escape them, which agree. The SQL function raises on a version it does not know and `chain.ts` refuses one; a later sub-project that adds a chained column adds version 2 to both, and every row keeps the version it was written with.
- `auth.append_audit(actor_id, action, target_type, target_id, metadata, staff_session, key_id, action_hash, step_up, request_id)` is owned by the migrator: `security definer`, `search_path` pinned to `auth, pg_temp`, `public.audit_log` named in full, EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC and granted to `korbat_auth` only. It refuses any action outside the auth side's list: `staff_key_revoked`, `staff_key_counter_regression`, `staff_sign_in`, `staff_signed_out`, `staff_session_revoked`, `staff_enrollments_cancelled` and `role_granted` (the take-down's revocations are `staff_key_revoked` rows). The auth flows call it as the last statement of their transaction.
- Migration 0012 is generated and then completed by hand in the same file, as `0009` is. Generated: the enum values, the auth tables with their checks, `audit_log`'s new columns with their placeholder defaults and its check, `audit_head` and the policies, with the unique index on `chain_seq` moved below the back-fill (created before it, it fails on the placeholder zeros). Hand-written, in this order: `audit_chain_text`; the existing rows' `created_at` truncated to milliseconds; the back-fill, which chains the existing rows in id order with `chain_version` 1, `chain_seq` from 1 and `prev_hash` from 64 zeros; the head row set to the last row's place and hash; the unique index; the REVOKE and GRANT on `audit_head`; `audit_chain` with EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC; the trigger; then the auth side: the REVOKE and GRANT on the staff tables, their never-cleared trigger, and `auth.add_staff_key`, `auth.append_audit` and `auth.forget_staff_keys` with EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC and granted to `korbat_auth`. drizzle's migrator runs it in one transaction, so a failure leaves nothing half-chained, and the generate gate still prints No schema changes.
- RLS on every new table with one policy: the auth tables' for `korbat_auth`; `audit_head`'s, SELECT only, for `korbat_api` (its owner, which the trigger runs as, bypasses RLS).

## 7. Flows

- **Enrolment.** `staff.js enroll <email>` runs in the migrate service as the migrator, needing only `DATABASE_URL_MIGRATE`, `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` and `CONSOLE_RP_ID`, which Compose gives that service. It refuses while `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` is unset, with a message naming it, and writes nothing. It refuses unless the account is live, staff and has a handle, and refuses an account that already has 5 live keys. It writes the enrolment, with `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` and the console's relying party ID recorded on it, the `staff_enrollment_issued` auth event and its audit row (system actor) in one transaction, and prints the link. The console's enrolment page asks for the key's name and its kind, then asks the API for registration options (`POST /api/staff/enroll/options { token, name, kind }`, where `kind` is `security_key` or `this_device`). The token must be unused and unexpired, and the account still live, staff and with a handle; otherwise the answer is `staff_enrollment_invalid`, as it is for an enrolment whose recorded origin or relying party ID is not the running configuration's (the console moved after the link was issued). The user handle is the account's `users.webauthn_user_id`, set now with 1c's compare-and-set (`ensureWebauthnUserId`) when the account has none. `user.name` and `user.displayName` are the key's name, never the email, the handle or the account id. The options name the console's relying party and require user verification and a resident key. They exclude the account's existing staff keys, and also its personal passkeys while `CONSOLE_RP_ID` equals the app's relying party ID (development), and 1c's registration options then exclude its live staff keys in the same way (section 2); and they pin the algorithms 1c pins. They carry the security-key hint when `kind` is `security_key` and no hint otherwise, and a `staff_register` challenge bound to the enrolment's id. `POST /api/staff/enroll/verify { token, name, response }` finds the enrolment by its token (`staff_enrollment_invalid` as above), reads the challenge against the enrolment's id and spends it, then verifies against `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` and `CONSOLE_RP_ID`. A challenge the codec refuses (forged, of another purpose, bound to another enrolment or spent), a response the library refuses and an attestation the wrapper does not accept are all `staff_key_refused`. It refuses a key whose registration did not report UV (`staff_key_not_user_verified`) or reported BE (`staff_key_not_device_bound`). A refusal leaves the link usable until it expires, so the person can try again with another key. Otherwise it does its whole write in one transaction on the auth role. The transaction first locks the account's row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (as the take-down and 1c's registration take it first; the lock's mode is under Command line below), writes the `staff_key_added` auth event, and last calls `auth.add_staff_key`, which re-checks under that lock that the account is live, staff and has a handle, that the enrolment still exists unused and unexpired, and that the account holds fewer than five live keys, then spends the token, stores the key with the AAGUID it reported and the origin and relying party ID the enrolment recorded, and writes its audit row. After the commit the owner is mailed `staff_key_added`. A link issued before a `revoke ... all`, a role change or a deletion therefore enrols nothing. At most 5 live keys per person: `staff.js enroll` refuses an account that already has 5, and the verify step refuses a sixth with `staff_key_limit` (when two links were issued at once).
- **Sign-in.** `POST /api/staff/signin/options` takes no body and answers with a `staff_signin` challenge, discoverable credentials, an empty allow list and user verification required. `POST /api/staff/signin/verify { response }` reads the challenge and spends it first. The credential must be a live staff key of a live staff account. The response's user handle must be present and equal to that account's `webauthn_user_id`, because the allow list named no one (as in 1c's `signInVerify`). Every failure up to here gets one answer: a challenge the codec refuses (forged, of another purpose or spent), an unknown or revoked key, an account that is no longer live or staff, and a missing or wrong user handle are all refused with `staff_key_invalid`. The library then verifies the signature, the origin `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` and the relying party ID, and a response it refuses is `staff_key_invalid` as well, so no answer tells a live key from a revoked or unknown one. After that the flow refuses an assertion that does not report UV (`staff_key_not_user_verified`) or that reports BE or BS (`staff_key_not_device_bound`; WebAuthn L3 asks a relying party that uses BE in policy to check it on every assertion), and it applies the counter rule: a regression, found only after the signature verifies, is refused with `staff_key_suspended` (below). It creates a staff session whose `browser` is `deviceLabel(userAgent, "web")`, sets the two cookies, writes `staff_sign_in` and its audit row in the session's transaction, and mails the owner `staff_session_started` with the key's name, the browser label and the time. Limits: per IP, 20 attempts an hour, counted before anything else. Per key, 10 an hour, counted only after the signature and the flags have checked, as 1c counts its new-device limit (`flows.ts:383-386`), so only the key's holder can spend it and an unsigned response naming a key's credential id cannot lock its owner out.
- **A counter that goes backwards.** Every staff assertion, at sign-in and at step-up, runs 1c's counter rule (`isCounterRegression`, one conditional update) after its signature has verified. A regression revokes the key. In one transaction on the auth role `suspect_at` and `revoked_at` are set, every live staff session the key opened is revoked, `staff_key_counter_regression` is appended, and its audit row is written through `auth.append_audit`, last. Then the owner is mailed `staff_key_suspect` (never 1c's `passkey_suspect`, whose remedy names the app's Security screen), and the attempt is refused with `staff_key_suspended`. It is the routine `staff.js revoke` runs, with `suspect_at` set as well and the regression's own event; a suspect key opens and confirms nothing more, and only the command line enrols a replacement.
- **The staff gate.** Every request under `/api/staff/`, with or without a session (sign-in and enrolment included), first passes one gate, before any flow runs or any session is read. Its `Host` header, port included, must equal `new URL(CONSOLE_ORIGIN).host`; otherwise it is answered 404 `not_found`, as on the app's site. The API reads `Host`, which Caddy and cloudflared pass on unchanged, never `X-Forwarded-Host`, and the integration harness sets `Host` itself, since an in-process request has none. An `Origin` header, when present, must be exactly `CONSOLE_ORIGIN`, and it must be present on anything but GET and HEAD. A `Sec-Fetch-Site` header, when present, must be `same-origin`; the API already reads fetch metadata (`apps/api/src/auth/routes.ts:61-67`). A request that fails the Origin or `Sec-Fetch-Site` test is answered 403 `csrf_failed` and touches no session, so a page on another origin of the same site (the app on a subdomain, or any localhost port in development) can neither act nor keep a staff session from going idle. Staff answers carry no CORS headers: the console calls its API on its own origin.
- **Every staff request.** `requireStaff` reads only the staff cookie (never a bearer token, never the app's session cookie) and, for anything but GET and HEAD, requires the `x-staff-csrf` header to equal the staff CSRF cookie. It reads the session from the database on every request, never from a cache: one indexed read joined to the key and the account, so a revocation, a demotion, a deletion or a suspect key ends the session at its next request with no tombstone. The session must be unrevoked and before its idle and absolute expiries, its key unrevoked, and its account live and still staff; otherwise the answer is 401 `staff_session_required`, which also clears both staff cookies (the console then shows sign-in). The idle expiry counts the person's actions, not requests: the console sends `X-Staff-Activity: 1` on each request a person's action starts (a click, a submitted form, opening a screen), and only such a request moves `last_used_at` to now and the idle expiry to the earlier of now plus 15 minutes and the absolute expiry, in the same statement that reads the session. A request without the header is checked the same way and moves nothing, so a page left open cannot keep its session alive by itself. The header governs the console's own requests; it is no defence against a script in the page, which the policy of section 8 and the step-up answer. Both staff cookies are set at sign-in with a Max-Age of 12 hours, the absolute limit, and are never refreshed; the server enforces the idle limit. Staff routes answer `Cache-Control: no-store` like every API answer.
- **Step-up.** `POST /api/staff/step-up/options { action, target, params }`: the action must be one of the contracts' step-up actions (`STAFF_STEP_UP_ACTIONS`; in 3a, `staff_session_revoked`), and `target` and `params` are validated with that action's own schema. The action's hash is the lower-case hex sha256 of the sorted JSON (object keys sorted, no whitespace) of `{ action, target: { type, id }, params }`. The answer carries a sealed `staff_step_up` challenge (the 1c pattern: an HMAC seal over its fields and an expiry, in the layout of section 5) whose binding is the full 32-byte sha256 of the session's handle, a newline and the action's hash, not a 16-byte prefix; the console checks that binding before it asks for the key (section 9). The options require user verification, and their allow list holds only the session's key. The challenge lives 10 minutes and the browser's prompt 5, as in every other ceremony (WebAuthn L3 recommends a timeout of 5 to 10 minutes). Single use and the binding, not a short life, keep an assertion from confirming anything else. The action route takes its own body with `stepUp: <assertion>` added. `requireStaffStepUp(action, target)` reads the route's own target from the request through its `target` function (for `DELETE /api/staff/sessions/:handle`, the path's handle), takes the params the route validated and will execute, and recomputes the action's hash from them. It reads the challenge against the session's handle and that hash (its seal, expiry and binding), then spends it (a used-challenge marker in Valkey until the expiry). It checks that the assertion's credential is the session's key and verifies it, refuses an assertion that does not report UV or reports BE or BS, and runs the counter rule (a regression as at sign-in). A challenge the codec refuses (forged, of another purpose, bound to another session, action, target or params, or spent), an assertion from another key and one the library refuses are all `staff_step_up_invalid`. The route's audit row, written in the same transaction as the action (through `auth.append_audit` when the action changes an auth table, as 3a's revocation does), stores the action's hash, `metadata.params`, the key and the assertion in `step_up`.
- **Sessions and keys.** `GET /api/staff/me` (the account's handle, role, the session's handle, and the session's idle and absolute expiries, each with the seconds left, so the console needs no clock of its own); `GET /api/staff/sessions` (the person's live staff sessions: browser, created, last used, this one); `DELETE /api/staff/sessions/:handle` with step-up (revokes one of the person's own staff sessions; its `staff_session_revoked` event and audit row); `POST /api/staff/signout` (ends this session; no step-up; `staff_signed_out` and its audit row); `GET /api/staff/keys` (the person's keys: name, created, last used; never the public key). `GET /api/staff/audit?action=staff_sign_in` (the audit reader's one filter) feeds the home screen's list of every staff account's sign-ins in the last 7 days; the console reads its pages until it reaches a sign-in older than 7 days. No route adds or revokes a key: keys are added through the command line's enrolments and revoked by the command line, a counter regression or an account's take-down.
- **The audit log.** `GET /api/staff/audit?cursor=` (any staff member; newest first by `chain_seq`, 50 a page, the cursor a `chain_seq`; `?at=<chain_seq>` answers the page whose newest row is at that place, which the keeper's check opens (section 2); each row: time, actor handle, action, target type and id, the action's hash, and its place and hash in the chain; never metadata that names an email, IP, device or phone, which the writer never stores). Reading is not itself recorded.
- **Command line.** `staff.js enroll|keys|revoke|audit-head|audit-verify`. `enroll` runs in the migrate service as the migrator; the others run like grant-role (`docker compose ... run --rm api node dist/staff.js ...`). `staff.js keys <email>` lists that account's keys (id, name, reported AAGUID, created, last used, revoked, suspect); without an email it lists every account whose role is not `user`, with its role, address, handle and keys, for the runbook's comparison with the operator's own list. `staff.js revoke <email> <key id>` revokes that key and every staff session it opened; `staff.js revoke <email> all` revokes every key and every staff session of the account and deletes its unused enrolments. Both run in one auth transaction that first locks the account's row and appends `staff_key_revoked` for each key revoked (metadata: the key's id, the count of staff sessions revoked, `via: "cli"`) with its audit row; `revoke ... all` also writes one `staff_enrollments_cancelled` row (the count, `via: "cli"`) when it deleted an unused enrolment, so it never changes the database without a row. After the commit the owner is mailed `staff_keys_revoked` when a key was revoked; an enrolment cancelled alone sends no mail, as with grant-role. The command line builds the mailer as the API does (`SMTP_URL`, `MAIL_FROM`); a mail that fails is printed as a warning and undoes nothing. Grant-role builds the same mailer for a demotion's `staff_keys_revoked`. Every writing command writes its audit row in the same transaction as its change: directly when running as the migrator, through `auth.append_audit` on the auth role. An audit row is always the last statement of its transaction: the head's lock lasts until commit, so nothing that can wait (another row lock, Valkey, a mail, a WebAuthn check) runs between the insert and the commit. A transaction that wrote its audit row first and then waited for a row another audit writer held would deadlock with it. Every transaction that locks an account's row before its other statements (enrolment and `auth.add_staff_key`, `staff.js revoke`, the counter regression) takes it `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, the lock that grant-role's and the take-down's updates of that row take. These still wait for each other, but the `FOR KEY SHARE` lock that an insert into `auth_events` or `staff_sessions` takes on the account's row through its foreign key does not wait for them. Taken `FOR UPDATE`, a revocation holding the account's row and waiting for a staff session's row would deadlock with a sign-out holding that session's row and inserting its event. `staff.js` and grant-role set `lock_timeout` to 10 seconds; a command that times out has written nothing, and says that an open transaction holds the audit head or the account's row and that the api service should be stopped before the command is run again. Grant-role's role change and its `role_granted` row become one transaction the same way (below), and it no longer writes the audit log on the API's pool. `staff.js audit-head` prints the newest row's `chain_seq`, hash and time, reading the newest row and `audit_head` in one statement and checking that the head names that row. `staff.js audit-verify [<chain_seq>:<hash> ...]` opens its own connection on the API's role with `DateStyle` ISO and `TimeZone` UTC and, in one `REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY` transaction, reads the head and every row up to the head's place in `chain_seq` order a page at a time, so a row written meanwhile is neither read nor missed. It then reads the staff keys (id, owner, public key, origin and relying party ID) from `auth.staff_keys` on the auth role; a key whose `staff_key_added` row is not among the rows read is looked up again, and one whose row was written after the head it read is left to the next run. It fails (exit 1, naming the first rows at fault) unless all of these hold: `chain_seq` runs from 1 without a gap; the first `prev_hash` is 64 zeros; every `prev_hash` is the previous row's hash; every hash matches the text `chain.ts` recomputes; `audit_head` names the newest row; every kept pair still names a row with that hash; every row with a step-up passes the checks below; every row whose action is in `STAFF_STEP_UP_ACTIONS` carries a step-up, whatever its actor; and every staff key has exactly one `staff_key_added` row, which names it in `key_id`. For every row with a step-up it recomputes the action's hash from the row's action, target and `metadata.params`; checks that the challenge in the client data carries the sha256 of the row's session handle and that hash, and had not expired when the row was written; checks that the key named by `key_id` belongs to the row's `actor_id`; checks that the client data is a `webauthn.get` for the origin recorded on the key and that the authenticator data names the relying party ID recorded on the key with user presence and verification; checks that the signature verifies with the key's public key; and checks that no challenge appears in two rows. It also reports how many rows follow the newest kept pair and are not yet anchored. It needs no server secret and no console configuration, so it runs the same with the console off or moved.
- **Role changes, deletion and retention.** `grantRole` (`apps/api/src/auth/admin.ts`) runs in one transaction on the auth role, starting with the role's update, which locks the account's row: it sets the role, revokes every session and every staff session, and deletes every unused enrolment. When the new role is `user` it also revokes every staff key and appends `staff_key_revoked` for each (`via: "role_change"`). Its last statements write, through `auth.append_audit`, the audit row of each `staff_key_revoked` and the `role_granted` row, whose metadata gains the counts of staff sessions revoked and enrolments deleted. After the commit the app sessions get their tombstones as today, and the owner is mailed `staff_keys_revoked` when a key was revoked. The account take-down (`takeDownRows`, `apps/api/src/auth/repo.ts:806`, which `finishTakeDown` runs again) also revokes the account's live staff keys and staff sessions, appending `staff_key_revoked` for each key (`via: "take_down"`), deletes its enrolments, and blanks its staff keys' names, AAGUIDs and transports through `auth.forget_staff_keys`, in the same transaction. `takeDownRows` writes no audit row: it returns the keys it revoked and the count of enrolments it deleted. `takeDown` after its `account_deleted` event (`repo.ts:229-230`), and `finishTakeDown` after `takeDownRows`, write through `auth.append_audit`, as their last statements, the audit row of each `staff_key_revoked` and, when an enrolment went, one `staff_enrollments_cancelled` row (the count, `via: "take_down"`). A deleted account keeps no live staff credential, as it keeps no passkey; its staff keys stay, revoked and without their names, AAGUIDs and transports, because `audit-verify` checks old confirmations against them. The staff sessions need no tombstone because they are never cached. The auth janitor (`apps/api/src/auth/janitor.ts`) deletes staff sessions 30 days after they were revoked or expired, as it deletes sessions, and enrolments a day after they were used or expired, as it deletes email codes; it never touches a live one. Staff keys are never deleted (audit rows name them by id). Staff auth events go after 90 days with every auth event; their audit rows stay.
- **Staff mails.** Four templates join the closed union (`apps/api/src/mail/mailer.ts`). They are plain paragraphs like the others, sent best effort through `notifyAccount` to the staff account's address, with times printed as 1c's mails print them. None carries a link, and each ends with 'korbat never puts a link in a staff email.' `staff_key_added` {name, time}: subject 'A staff key was added to your korbat staff account'; 'A staff key named "{name}" was registered for your korbat staff account at {time}. It can open the staff console.' 'If this was you, there is nothing to do. If not, tell the operator at once; they revoke the key with the staff command line.' `staff_session_started` {name, browser, time}: subject 'A staff session started on the korbat console'; 'A staff session was started on the korbat console at {time} with your staff key "{name}", from {browser}.' 'If this was you, there is nothing to do. If not, someone holds that key: tell the operator at once; they revoke it and every session it opened with the staff command line.' `staff_key_suspect` {name}: subject 'A staff key on your korbat staff account was switched off'; 'A use of your staff key "{name}" on the korbat console (a sign-in or a confirmation) looked like a copy of it, so korbat switched that key off and ended every staff session it opened. It can no longer open the console.' 'Ask the operator for a link to register a new key. If you did not use it just now, tell them so: someone else holds a copy of the key.' `staff_keys_revoked` {names, time}: subject 'Staff keys revoked on your korbat staff account'; 'These staff keys of your korbat staff account were revoked at {time}: {names}. They can no longer open the console, and every staff session they opened has ended.' 'If you expected this, there is nothing to do. If not, ask the operator why.' No mail is sent when someone signs out, revokes one of their own sessions in the console, or changes a role in a way that revokes no key, and an account's take-down sends only its `account_deleted` mail.

## 8. Origins, hosts and headers

- `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` is read like `WEBAUTHN_RP_ID`: a blank value counts as unset, since Compose passes an empty line of `infra/.env` as an empty string. Unset, the console is off: the staff routes are not mounted, every path under `/api/staff/` answers 404 on either site, and `staff.js enroll` refuses. Caddy's console site is always built and always serves the page, which says that the console is not available at this address (section 9) when `GET /api/staff/me` answers 404; the API is the only switch, and no tunnel hostname reaches the site until the operator adds one. In every environment `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` must not equal `APP_ORIGIN` and must not appear in `TRUSTED_ORIGINS`. Listing it there would make it an allowed Origin for the app's cookie-authenticated writes, a credentialed CORS origin of the app's API and, outside production, an accepted origin for personal passkeys (`apps/api/src/auth/context.ts:40,53`). In production it must be https. Its hostname must differ from `APP_ORIGIN`'s hostname, because ports do not separate sites or cookies. `APP_ORIGIN`'s hostname must not be a subdomain of it either, since a console on a parent domain of the app would let the app's pages ask for staff keys. `CONSOLE_RP_ID` defaults to the console's hostname. In production it must equal that hostname and differ from the app's relying party ID, so staff keys work on the console and nowhere else. These relying-party rules apply in production only, as the app's do (`apps/api/src/config.ts:131`).
- Caddy's console site listens on port 8090 in the container (the image also `EXPOSE`s 8090). The development override publishes it on `127.0.0.1:${DEV_CONSOLE_PORT:-8090}`, and `CONSOLE_ORIGIN` must name the same port on localhost. The site proxies `/api/staff` and `/api/staff/*` to `api:3000` as the app's site proxies `/api/*`: `request_body` at 64KB, `header -Server`, and `import /etc/caddy/cf-header.caddy` inside `reverse_proxy`, so a client-set `CF-Connecting-IP` is dropped unless `TUNNEL=on` (the per-IP sign-in limits key on it). It sets no `header_up Host`, so the API sees the Host the browser sent. Caddy's own handling of `X-Forwarded-For`, `X-Forwarded-Proto` and `X-Forwarded-Host`, which replaces what an untrusted client sent, is kept. Any other `/api/*` is answered 404. Everything else is served from the console's static files, with `index.html` as the fallback and `encode gzip`. They live in a directory outside the app's root (`/console`, not under `/srv`), so the app's site can never serve them. Its headers: the CSP of section 3 exactly, `Strict-Transport-Security`, `X-Content-Type-Options nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options DENY`, `Referrer-Policy no-referrer`, `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy same-origin`, `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy same-origin`, `Permissions-Policy` denying camera, microphone and geolocation and allowing `publickey-credentials-get` and `publickey-credentials-create` to the console itself only, `Cache-Control: no-store, no-transform` on the page (every answer that is not a hashed bundle file), and `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` on the hashed files. `no-transform` stops Cloudflare injecting its JavaScript Detections script, which Bot Fight Mode turns on for a whole zone and which cannot be turned off, and its automatic Web Analytics beacon. The CSP would block either on every page view. The app's site answers `/api/staff` and `/api/staff/*` with 404, ahead of its API proxy.
- In development both origins are on localhost: the app at `http://localhost:8080` through Caddy or `http://localhost:8081` from Expo's dev server, the console at `http://localhost:8090`. Browsers send each origin's cookies to every port of localhost, so the app's cookies reach the console's port and the staff cookies reach the app's. Four things keep the two apart: the separate cookie names, the staff gate (Host, Origin and `Sec-Fetch-Site`), the staff CSRF header and the absence of CORS on staff routes. Both relying party IDs are `localhost` there, so a browser offers personal passkeys at the console's sign-in and staff keys at the app's, and only the tables refuse them. The console is opened at `http://localhost:<port>`, never `127.0.0.1`, because browsers refuse WebAuthn on an IP address. In production the hostnames differ.

## 9. The console app (`apps/admin`)

React function components, a small router of its own (four paths: `/signin`, `/enroll/:token`, `/`, `/audit`), `fetch` wrappers that send the CSRF header and parse every answer with the contracts' zod schemas (jitless), one CSS file. Every user-written string (handles, key names, browser names) renders as a text node; no `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, no `innerHTML`, no links built from data.

Screens: sign-in ("Sign in with your staff key", the browser's prompt, errors in the words of section 10); home (signed in as, role, the staff sign-ins of every staff account in the last 7 days (time and handle, newest first, read page by page until a sign-in older than 7 days), the session's absolute end ("Signed in until 21:40"), the person's staff sessions with "Sign out that session" confirmed by the key, the person's keys); the audit log (a table, newest first, "Load older", and "Go to place", which opens the page at a place the keeper kept); "Cases arrive in sub-project 3c." When `GET /api/staff/me` answers 404, the console shows only "The staff console is not available at this address. It is off on this server, or this is not the address set in CONSOLE_ORIGIN: open it at exactly that address, never at 127.0.0.1." The staff gate answers a wrong Host with the same 404 as a console that is off, so the page cannot tell the two apart.

Before it asks for the key to confirm an action, the console checks the step-up options it was given. The challenge must be the 74 bytes of the step-up layout (the contracts' offsets) with the `staff_step_up` purpose, its binding must equal the sha256 of the session's handle (from `GET /api/staff/me`), a newline and the action's hash that the page computes with WebCrypto from the `{ action, target, params }` it sent, and the allow list must hold one key. Otherwise the page does not call `navigator.credentials.get` and says the confirmation could not be checked. So an API under an attacker's control cannot have the key sign an action the person did not choose; the check trusts the page, which Caddy serves from its image, not the API.

Enrolment. Before the ceremony the page says which keys work: a security key with a PIN, or Windows Hello on this computer. It says which will be refused: passkeys saved by a password manager, such as iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager, and a phone's own passkeys. It says how to set a PIN on a new security key when the browser does not offer to. It asks for the key's name, and says the key will show that name, so it should not be an email or a real name. It offers two buttons. "Register a security key" sends `kind: security_key`. "Use Windows Hello on this computer" sends `kind: this_device`, and the page says to choose Windows Hello or this device in the browser's dialog. Each refusal is explained in words: `staff_key_not_device_bound` means the browser saved a synced passkey, `staff_key_not_user_verified` means the key asked for no PIN or fingerprint, and `staff_key_refused` means the browser's answer could not be checked. The page says the link still works. After either of the first two refusals, and only when the enrolment options say that the console's relying party ID is not the app's (`rpShared` false), the page calls `PublicKeyCredential.signalUnknownCredential({ rpId, credentialId })` for the credential it has just created, where the browser has the call (Chrome and Edge 132+, Safari 26), so the password manager drops the refused passkey. Where the browser lacks the call, or the two IDs are shared, the page tells the person to delete the console's entry from their password manager by hand. The WebKit bugs that kept 1c from the Signal API (322267 and 302624) delete other passkeys only on the same relying party ID, and outside development the console's ID holds no synced passkey that korbat accepts; in development both IDs are `localhost`, where the call could delete the developer's app passkeys, so the page does not make it there. Sign-in never calls it, because every failure before the signature gets one answer. A failed sign-in says to choose the security key or Windows Hello rather than a password manager's passkey.

The console sends `X-Staff-Activity: 1` on every request a person's action starts, and on no other. Its only timed requests are the two reads of its idle timer, which carry no mark. The timer restarts at 15 minutes, or at the time left to the session's absolute end when that is less, whenever an activity-marked request succeeds. Two minutes before it runs out (at 13 minutes of a full idle period) the console reads `GET /api/staff/me`: when more than two minutes are left (the person acted in another tab of the same session), it sets the timer from the answer's seconds left; otherwise it shows, in a live region, "Your staff session ends in 2 minutes." with a "Stay signed in" button that sends one activity-marked `GET /api/staff/me`. When the absolute end is the nearer one, the warning instead reads "Your staff session ends at 21:40. Sign in again with your staff key then." and has no button, since nothing moves the absolute end. When the timer runs out it reads `/me` once more and either sets the timer from the answer or shows sign-in. Any `staff_session_required` answer shows the sign-in screen in place with "Your staff session ended" and keeps the path; after the sign-in the console returns to that path. A later screen that refreshes itself (3c's queue) sends its refreshes without the mark, and 3c keeps a draft in memory across a sign-in by the same account.

The build writes `dist/index.html` and hashed bundle and stylesheet files; esbuild's options pin the target and minify.

## 10. Contracts

`packages/contracts/src/staff.ts` holds: the enrolment, sign-in and step-up requests and answers (WebAuthn JSON through the existing schemas; the enrolment options request is `{ token, name, kind }`, with `kind` one of `security_key` and `this_device`, and its answer also says whether the console's relying party ID is the app's (`rpShared`); the step-up options request is `{ action, target, params }`); `STAFF_STEP_UP_ACTIONS`, each step-up action's name, target type and the zod schema of its params, read by the step-up options, `requireStaffStepUp`, `audit-verify` and the console (3a: `staff_session_revoked`, a staff session as target, params `{}`); the action's sorted-JSON text and the step-up challenge's layout (its offsets and purpose code), which the API, `audit-verify` and the console share; the stored assertion's shape; `StaffMe` (with the session's handle, `idleSecondsLeft` and `absoluteSecondsLeft`), `StaffSession` (with `browser`), `StaffKey`, `AuditEntry` and `AuditPage` (with the `at` request); and `StaffErrorCode`.

`StaffErrorCode` lists `staff_session_required`, `staff_key_invalid`, `staff_key_suspended`, `staff_key_limit`, `staff_key_not_device_bound`, `staff_key_not_user_verified`, `staff_key_refused`, `staff_enrollment_invalid`, `staff_step_up_required` and `staff_step_up_invalid`. `staff_key_invalid` covers an unknown or revoked key, an account that is no longer live or staff, a sign-in challenge the codec refuses and a response the library refuses, with one answer for all. The auth module raises these codes: `AuthErrorKind` becomes `AuthErrorCode | StaffErrorCode | "invalid_request"`, and `MESSAGES` (`apps/api/src/auth/context.ts`) gains their messages, so `app.ts`'s error handler needs no new branch. Staff routes may also answer the shared `challenge_expired`, `rate_limited` and `invalid_request`, and the staff gate's `not_found` and `csrf_failed`. They never answer a `passkey_*` code, whose messages send people to remedies the console does not have: the staff flows catch the codec's and the wrapper's `passkey_invalid` and `passkey_unsupported` and raise `staff_key_invalid` at sign-in, `staff_step_up_invalid` at step-up and `staff_key_refused` at enrolment. Where only the operator can help, the messages say so.

- `staff_session_required`: "Your staff session ended. Sign in with your staff key."
- `staff_key_invalid`: "This key does not open the staff console. If it should, ask the operator to check your staff keys."
- `staff_key_suspended`: "This staff key was switched off because a copy of it may exist. Ask the operator for a link to enrol a new one."
- `staff_key_limit`: "This account has 5 staff keys. Ask the operator to revoke one before enrolling another."
- `staff_key_not_device_bound`: "This key can be synced or copied, so it cannot be a staff key. Use a security key or a device-bound authenticator."
- `staff_key_not_user_verified`: "This key did not ask for a PIN or fingerprint, so it cannot be a staff key. Set a PIN on it, or use another key."
- `staff_key_refused`: "The browser's answer for this key could not be checked. Try again, or use another key; this link still works."
- `staff_enrollment_invalid`: "This enrolment link has expired or was already used. Ask the operator for a new one."
- `staff_step_up_required`: "Confirm this with your staff key."
- `staff_step_up_invalid`: "That confirmation was for another action, was already used or could not be checked. Try again."

The console words every code it can receive from a table of its own, using these texts.

## 11. Security and privacy

What stays impossible even for an admin, and what proves it (`threats.md` 3.3.1): no route changes a role (a route-table test); no route acts as another user or reads another user's sessions, email, IP, device or phone (a route-table test and the architecture rule); no audit row can be changed, removed or placed out of the chain by the API role (database tests: UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE on `audit_log`; any write to or lock on `audit_head`; `session_replication_role`; disabling the trigger; a temporary `audit_head`; an insert naming an existing id; supplied chain columns), and a change by the owner `korbat_migrate` or the superuser to rows a kept head covers is detected by `audit-verify` against that head; no staff power without a staff session, on the console's origin, from a staff key whose authenticator reported UV and no BE (integration tests with the software authenticator, including a key reporting BE refused at enrolment and at sign-in, a key reporting no UV refused, a response without the owner's user handle refused, a personal passkey refused, an assertion from the app's origin refused, an app session cookie refused, a bearer token refused); no staff answer readable or actionable from another origin: staff routes carry no CORS headers, the staff gate refuses a foreign Origin or a `Sec-Fetch-Site` other than same-origin on every method, and the configuration refuses the console's origin in the app's trusted origins (HTTP and configuration tests); no action confirmed by an assertion made for another (a step-up test), and no key asked to sign a step-up whose challenge binds another action than the one the person chose (the console's tests and the browser check); no staff key or staff session left live, and no unused enrolment left, after a demotion or a deletion, and no key enrolled by a link issued before a `revoke ... all`, a demotion or a deletion (integration tests); no page left open keeping a staff session past 15 minutes without an action by its person (an integration test and the console's timer tests). No staff key exists without an enrolment the command line issued, and no code in the API can add one, delete one or revive a revoked or suspect one (database tests as `korbat_auth`). No row of a step-up action is in the record, whatever its actor, without an assertion that `audit-verify` checks against a key of that row's actor (audit tests), and no staff session pairs an account with another account's key (a database test). Every auth-side fact commits or rolls back together with its audit row (an audit test). Rows without a step-up, such as sign-ins and sign-outs, remain the API's word.

What the flags cannot prove: without attestation, UV and BE are claims the authenticator makes about itself, so a software authenticator that reports BE=0 while its key can be copied would pass. That residual is left to three things. The runbook lists the authenticators staff use: a FIDO2 security key with a PIN, or Windows Hello on a computer with a TPM. The operator hands over each enrolment link. `staff.js keys` prints each key's reported AAGUID, which is zeros when the browser or the authenticator withholds it.

Staff keys are never shown in full; the audit reader never returns metadata that names a person beyond the actor's handle.

- **The enrolment page signs in with the new key itself (sections 2 and 9; found by the review).** Its "Sign in with this key" button narrows the allowed credentials to the key just registered and signs in whatever session the browser holds, so the check after each enrolment always uses the new key.
- **A confirmed action never lands after its expiry (sections 2 and 7; found by the review).** The confirmed revocation's transaction sets `lock_timeout` to the lesser of 15 seconds and a third of the challenge's remaining life by the database's clock, and the statement that writes its audit row reads the database's clock after the insert and undoes everything when the challenge has expired; a late confirmation writes nothing and answers `challenge_expired`. The one-minute read margin no longer assumes the API's and the database's clocks agree.
- **`auth.forget_staff_keys` blanks only revoked keys (section 6; found by the review).** Migration 0013 restates it so it blanks the name, AAGUID and transports only of keys whose `revoked_at` is set, of an account marked deleted; the take-down revokes them earlier in the same transaction, and code in the API gains nothing it cannot already do by revoking.
- **Revocation order and grant-role's after-commit steps (section 7, Role changes; found by the review).** Demotion and take-down revoke keys before staff sessions, as `staff.js revoke` does, so a concurrent sign-in cannot keep an unrevoked session; grant-role's cache tombstones and its mail run as independent best-effort steps after the commit, each reported as a warning, and the command exits 0 once the change is committed.
- **Smaller fixes (sections 7 and 9; found by the review).** `staff.js keys` reads in read-only transactions with a 10-second lock timeout; the console reads a marked `/me` right before the step-up options and every `/me` answer replaces the page's session; Go to place says when there is no row at the place asked for; an older Load older's answer never joins a page opened after it; the browser check's fixtures answer with the API's own statuses.
